## PART 1: LONG-FORM VIDEO VIRALITY

### How YouTube's algorithm works for long-form

YouTube distributes videos through two main surfaces:
1. **Search** — someone types a query, your video appears
2. **Suggested / Browse** — YouTube recommends your video to people watching related content

The algorithm optimises for **session time** — it recommends videos that keep people on YouTube longer. It doesn't care about views. It cares about: did people click? Did they watch? Did they keep watching after?

**The two metrics that determine everything:**

| Metric | Target | What it means |
|--------|--------|--------------|
| Click-Through Rate (CTR) | > 5% (strong: > 8%) | Of everyone who saw the thumbnail, what % clicked? |
| Average View Duration (AVD) | > 50% of video length | Of people who clicked, how much did they watch? |

High CTR + low AVD = clickbait. YouTube penalises this — pushes the video to fewer people over time.
Low CTR + high AVD = good content, poor discovery. The title/thumbnail isn't doing its job.
High CTR + high AVD = algorithm amplification. YouTube pushes this everywhere.

**The third metric that triggers breakout:**
- **Non-subscriber views %** — what % of your viewers are NOT already subscribers. If this goes above 50%, YouTube is actively distributing you to new audiences. Target: > 40%.

---

### Thumbnail formula

The thumbnail is seen before the title. It is the first impression — it has ~0.5 seconds to communicate something compelling enough to stop the scroll.

**What works for each niche:**

**@breathofdatascience:**
- Your face (reaction/surprise expression) + a code snippet or error message on screen
- High contrast: dark background, bright text
- Maximum 3–4 words of text, large font
- The text should amplify the reaction, not explain the video: "I trusted it for 6 days" over a confused-face photo performs better than "Python type errors explained"
- Avoid: cluttered screenshots, tiny text, no face

**@breathoflife_ and @breathofpoetry:**
- Your face (emotion that matches the content) + minimal text
- Warm, natural colour grade — not oversaturated
- Text: the hook phrase from the title, or the most emotionally resonant line
- For poetry: atmospheric backgrounds + text-on-screen work better than face-forward thumbnails

**Thumbnail A/B testing:**
Upload two thumbnails, wait 48 hours, check CTR in YouTube Studio. Keep the winner. If both are below 4%, redo the thumbnail — it's the single highest-leverage change you can make.

### Title formula

The title does two jobs: (1) rank in YouTube search, (2) trigger curiosity for suggested video viewers.

**For @breathofdatascience — search-optimised titles:**
Include the keyword a data scientist would actually search, then add the hook:
- "Python Type Errors: The Bug That Gave Me Confidently Wrong Data for 6 Days"
- "Pandas Merge Explained — Why Your Joins Are Silently Dropping Rows"
- "Machine Learning Accuracy Is Lying to You — Here's What to Check Instead"

Formula: `[Search keyword]: [Specific incident or counter-intuitive result]`

**For @breathoflife_ — curiosity-driven titles:**
No search intent here — people don't search for personal essays. The title must be compelling enough to stop the scroll in suggested:
- "I White-Knuckled Life for Three Years. Here's What the Silence Was Hiding."
- "My Friend Called Me at Midnight. I Gave Him Budgeting Tips."
- "The Lie We Inherited About Strength"

Formula: `[Specific incident or named observation that creates tension]`

**For @breathofpoetry — hybrid (poem title + emotional hook):**
- "Intoxicated Senses — A Poem About the Hangover That Love Leaves"
- "The Madness That Doesn't Announce Itself | Spoken Word"

Formula: `[Poem title] — [What the poem is actually about, emotionally]`

**Title rules across all channels:**
- No clickbait that the video doesn't deliver on — YouTube tracks "impression clicks" that stop early, and this suppresses the video
- No ALL CAPS (aggressive, low-trust)
- 60 characters max (anything beyond is truncated in mobile)
- Put the keyword or most compelling phrase first — titles truncate from the right

### Script structure — the retention framework

YouTube Analytics shows you an exact retention graph: where people watch, where they pause, and where they leave. The goal is to keep the line as flat as possible.

**First 30 seconds — the hook (most important)**
Retention drops fastest in the first 30 seconds. The first words of the video must be the hook — the specific incident, counter-intuitive result, or named moment. No "welcome back," no title card monologue, no context-setting.

Wrong opening: "Hey everyone, today we're going to be talking about Python type errors. These are really common mistakes that a lot of data scientists make, so in this video I'm going to walk you through..."

Right opening: "Python gave me a perfectly confident wrong number. No error. No crash. No warning. I trusted it for six days."

**2–3 minute mark — pattern interrupt**
This is where a second retention drop occurs — the viewer has decided they like the video but needs re-engagement. Insert at the 2–3 minute mark:
- A surprising sub-point that contradicts what you just said
- A live demo or screen transition (DS)
- A personal story beat (Life/Poetry)
- A visual change (cut to screen recording, b-roll, or motion graphic)

**End screen setup — session time extension**
The last 20 seconds: add two end screen cards pointing to:
1. Your most-watched video on this channel (keeps session time high)
2. Your playlist for this series (series compounding)

Say aloud: "If you want [next related topic], that video is right there." Point at the card on screen.

**Series compounding — the most underused YouTube tactic**
Each DS tutorial ends with a tease of next week's piece: "Next week — the pandas merge bug that corrupted 6 months of production data, silently. Subscribe so you don't miss it." This creates a pre-warmed audience that returns. Over 4–6 weeks, this compounds into a subscriber base that expects your content.

## PART 2: YOUTUBE SHORTS VIRALITY

### How YouTube Shorts algorithm works

Shorts are distributed primarily through the **Shorts feed** — a vertical scroll similar to TikTok. YouTube shows each Short to a sample of viewers; if engagement is strong, it pushes to more.

**The two signals YouTube Shorts optimises for:**
1. **Like/view ratio** — higher than long-form; target > 5%
2. **Comments per view** — comments signal strong reaction (positive or negative). A Short with 10 comments per 1,000 views will be pushed harder than one with 1,000 views and 0 comments.

**The loop-ability signal:**
If a viewer watches a Short more than once (the Short loops), that is a strong positive signal. Design Shorts so the ending flows naturally back into the beginning — this is the loop mechanic. It doesn't need to be a perfect loop; it just needs to not feel dead-ended.

---

### Shorts structure — the 5-beat viral formula

Every Short follows this structure (maximum 60 seconds):

| Beat | Seconds | What happens |
|------|---------|-------------|
| Hook | 0–3s | First words are the specific hook. No preamble. This decides whether they swipe away. |
| Problem | 3–15s | Establish the specific tension or gap. Make them feel the problem. |
| Reveal + proof | 15–35s | The counter-intuitive insight or solution, shown — not just told. Show the code running, show the result, say the poem line. |
| Payoff | 35–50s | The specific takeaway or emotional resolution. The shareable moment. |
| CTA | 50–60s | One clear action. DS/Life: "Comment [KEYWORD] and I'll send you the full breakdown." Poetry: "Save this if it found you at the right time 🤍" |

**Only one CTA per Short.** Not "like, comment, subscribe, and follow." One action. The algorithm rewards comment keyword triggers because they generate comments (signal) and DMs (off-platform engagement).

---

### Hook formulas by niche

**@breathofdatascience Shorts hooks:**
- "Python gave me a perfectly confident wrong number. No error. No crash."
- "This one pandas line looks completely fine. It's been silently dropping your data."
- "I deployed a model with 91% accuracy. It predicted the same class for everything."

**@breathoflife_ Shorts hooks:**
- "Break a bone and people hold the door for you. Tell someone you haven't slept in 3 weeks — the room goes quiet."
- "I called my parents when I was drowning. They gave me a budget."
- "I didn't realise I was the problem until I was the only constant."

**@breathofpoetry Shorts hooks:**
- "There's a specific kind of madness that doesn't announce itself."
- "Love has taken away all my senses — like that of alcohol."
- "One day you're fine. The next, someone's laugh is living rent-free in your head."

**Hook rules:**
- Must be said in the first 3 seconds
- Must contain a specific number, result, or named sensation
- Must be delivered looking directly at camera
- Must also appear as a text overlay on screen at 0:00 — 40% of Shorts are watched with sound off

---

### Text overlays — mandatory for Shorts

Three text overlays are non-negotiable in every Short:

1. **Hook line at 0:00** — large, high-contrast, readable in 1 second
2. **Shareable moment** — the single most quotable line from the Short, shown mid-video
3. **CTA line** — "Comment [KEYWORD] for the full breakdown" or "Save this 🤍"

Everything else is optional. These three are not.

**Remotion workflow (automated):**
The three mandatory overlays are driven by `[TEXT_OVERLAY]` tags in the script file. Pipeline:
1. Tags annotated in script on Tuesday (`[TEXT_OVERLAY: shown at 0:00]`, `[TEXT_OVERLAY: shareable moment]`, `[TEXT_OVERLAY: CTA]`)
2. `generate_scene_plans.py --mode overlay` reads the tags → produces `remotion/public/scene-plans/{week}/{slug}_overlay.json`
3. `npx remotion render --composition VerticalReel --props ...overlay.json` bakes overlays onto the vertical clip → `assets/hyperframes/{week}/{slug}_reel_final.mp4`
4. Trending audio is added natively in the Instagram app at post time (Audio button during Reel posting) — do not bake into the file.

---

### Shorts analytics to track every Sunday

Go to YouTube Studio → Content → filter to Shorts:

| Metric | Target | What to do |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Views per Short | Growing week-over-week | — |
| Like/view ratio | > 5% | If below 5%, hook or payoff failed |
| Comments per Short | > 1% of views | If below 1%, CTA wasn't compelling enough |
| Average % viewed | > 80% | If below 70%, drop-off point = where you lost them |
| Non-subscriber views | > 50% | If below 40%, only your existing audience is watching — algorithm isn't distributing |

**The one decision rule:**
If a Short gets > 80% average viewed AND > 1% comment rate → this hook format and topic angle work. Replicate it within 2 weeks on the same channel.
