### My voice
Warm, personal, specific. I speak like a person, not a presenter. No performance. No "Welcome back to the show, I'm your host Tarun." The first words of every episode are the hook — the same hook used in the YouTube video.
**Banned phrases:**
- "Welcome back to another episode of…"
- "Today we're going to be talking about…"
- "If you're new here, make sure to subscribe"
- "Like and subscribe" (podcast equivalent: "Leave a review")
- "Let me know in the comments" (wrong platform)
- "In conclusion"

### How Spotify's algorithm works
Spotify recommends podcasts based on three signals, in order of weight:
**1. Episode completion rate (most important)**
If listeners finish your episodes, Spotify pushes you to similar listeners. If they drop off at 30%, Spotify stops recommending you. Target: completion rate > 65% per episode.
What kills completion rate:
- Slow openers (more than 60 seconds before the actual content starts)
- Long ad reads or self-promotional breaks mid-episode
- Pacing that is flat — no variation in energy or emotional register
- No "reason to stay" planted early (the listener must know within 90 seconds what they'll get by the end)
What improves completion rate:
- Starting the episode with the hook — the most compelling moment, said first
- Planting a question or tension in the first 60 seconds that only resolves at the end
- Varying pace — slower for emotional moments, faster for energy moments
- Episodes that feel complete and don't end abruptly
**2. Listener-to-follower conversion rate**
When a new listener plays an episode and then follows the show, that's a strong signal. Spotify increases recommendations when this happens consistently. This is driven almost entirely by the first episode someone hears — if it's good, they follow. If it's average, they don't.
The implication: your most-shared episode is your most important one, because it's what new listeners hear first.
**3. Saves to library**
When a listener saves an episode to their library (the Spotify equivalent of a bookmark), that is a strong quality signal. A save indicates: "I want to come back to this."

### Episode title formula
Spotify shows the episode title in search results, in the "Your daily podcasts" shelf, and in episode recommendations. The title determines whether someone taps play.
The same hook rules that apply to Instagram and Medium apply here. The episode title must contain one of:
- **A specific incident or result** — "The Night I Called My Parents and Asked for Help — They Gave Me a Budget"
- **A specific counter-intuitive observation** — "The More I 'Worked on Myself', the More I Lost Myself"
- **A named emotional experience** — "The Quiet Dread That Stays After the Deadline Passes"
Title anti-patterns to avoid:
- Episode numbers as titles ("Episode 14") — never use this alone
- Vague topic labels ("Mental Health", "About Anxiety") — no tension, no reason to click
- Question titles with obvious answers ("Is Self-Care Really Enough?") — readers already know the answer
**Life show title examples that work:**
- "I White-Knuckled Life for Three Years. Here's What I Was Really Running From."
- "I Called My Parents When I Was Drowning in Anxiety. They Gave Me Budgeting Tips."
- "The Lie We Inherited About Strength"
**Poetry show title examples that work:**
- "Intoxicated Senses — A Poem About the Hangover That Won't Lift"
- "The Madness That Doesn't Announce Itself"
- "When Love Starts Feeling Like Physics"

### Episode description formula
Spotify shows the first 1–2 sentences of the episode description in search previews. Those lines are the hook — same rule as the Medium first paragraph.
**Structure:**
- Line 1: The hook (same as or adapted from the episode title)
- Line 2–3: What the listener will feel or realise by the end (not what you cover — what they experience)
- Line 4: "Full piece on Substack: [URL]"
- Line 5: Follow the show for weekly episodes
**Example (Life episode):**
```
I called my parents when I was drowning in anxiety at work. They listed budgeting tips.
They weren't wrong. They were practical. They passed down the only framework they had. But I wasn't calling for strategy.
This episode is about the gap between what we need and what we're trained to give each other — and where mental health stigma actually lives.
Full piece: medium.com/@tarun-gupta/the-lie-we-inherited-about-strength
Follow Breath of Life for new episodes every week.
```
**Example (Poetry episode):**
```
There's a specific kind of madness that doesn't announce itself. One day you're fine. The next, someone's laugh is living rent-free in your head.
This is a reading of "Intoxicated Senses" — a poem about the hangover that love leaves behind.
Full piece: medium.com/@tarun-gupta/intoxicated-senses
Follow Breath of Poetry for weekly poems and readings.
```

### Cross-promotion strategy — how to grow the podcast
The podcast grows from YouTube and Instagram, not from within Spotify. Nobody discovers podcasts by browsing Spotify. They follow because someone they trust recommended it.
**YouTube → Podcast funnel (most important):**
- Every Life and Poetry YouTube video ends with: "This episode is also a podcast on Spotify — follow Breath of [Life/Poetry] if you prefer listening."
- The description of every YouTube video includes: "🎙️ Podcast version: [Spotify show link]"
**Instagram → Podcast funnel:**
- Every Life and Poetry reel caption ends with: "Also a podcast on Spotify — follow Breath of [Life/Poetry]"
- Once a month: post an audiogram clip (the 30-second most emotionally resonant moment from the episode, visualised as a waveform over a still image) as a dedicated Instagram post
**Medium → Podcast funnel:**
- Every Life and Poetry Medium post ends with: "Prefer listening? This piece is available as a podcast episode on Spotify."

### Metrics to track every Sunday
Go to Spotify for Podcasters → Analytics for each show. Note:
| Metric | Where | Target |
|--------|-------|--------|
| Streams | Overview → Streams | Growing week-over-week |
| Listeners | Overview → Listeners | Unique listeners (distinct from streams) |
| Completion rate | Episode detail → Performance | > 65% |
| Followers | Audience → Followers | Growing |
| Saves | Episode detail | Any saves = strong signal |
**Decision rule:**
- Completion rate < 50% on an episode → the opening didn't hook or the episode ran too long. Check the drop-off timestamp in analytics — that's exactly where you lost them.
- Completion rate > 70% → this episode's structure worked. Replicate the opening format next week.
- A spike in followers after a specific episode → that episode is your best acquisition tool. Share it again on Instagram and in Substack.

- Breath of Poetry: 137 all-time plays, 0 followers, dormant since April 2024
- Last 30 days: 0 plays on both shows

**Relaunch-phase targets (first 8 episodes back):**
These replace the steady-state targets above until both shows have 4+ consecutive weekly episodes with data.

| Metric | Steady-state target | Relaunch target |
|--------|--------------------|----|
| Completion rate | > 65% | > 50% (algorithm is cold — any completion is positive signal) |
| Follower conversion | Measurable growth | Any net positive |
| Saves | Any saves | Not a priority yet |
| 30-day plays | Growing | > 0 (the bar is just restarting) |

**Relaunch priorities in order:**
1. Publish consistently — weekly without gaps. Spotify deprioritises shows that stop.
2. Re-share Life ep 8 (153 plays) on Instagram the same week ep 12 goes live.
3. Check Poetry ep 11 audio (0 consumption on 18 plays = likely broken upload).
4. Do NOT optimise for completion rate until you have 4 episodes of data.
5. Cross-promo CTAs (YouTube description → Spotify) matter more than episode structure right now — getting new listeners to the show page is the bottleneck, not retention.

**When to exit relaunch phase:**
When Breath of Life has 4 consecutive weekly episodes each with > 20 plays and measurable consumption time. Then apply steady-state virality framework fully.
