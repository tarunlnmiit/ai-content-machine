Create a reflective worksheet PDF (A4, portrait) for "Your 5-Minute Signal: A One-Week Data Audit"

Layout:
- Header (top 15%): Soft gradient background. Title + subtitle about personal growth.
- 4 sections vertically stacked, each with:
  - Section title (warm orange or teal accent)
  - Reflective prompt in italics
  - Blank space for handwritten response (30-50% of section height)
  - Dividing line between sections

Colors: Warm cream background (#FFFBF0), teal accents (#0D9488), dark gray text

Sections:
1. The Consumption Audit
   - List every self-improvement input you consumed last week (podcasts, YouTube, newsletters, books). Be specific — titles, approximate time.
   - For each item, write one thing you actually did differently because of it. If nothing changed, write 'nothing.'
   - What's the ratio of consuming to doing in your typical week? What does that number tell you?

2. Your Three-Line Morning Practice
   - For the next 7 days, write these three things before opening your phone — what you're worried about today, what you want to accomplish, and one honest sentence about how you're feeling. Set a two-minute timer.
   - After day 3, read back your entries. What surprised you? What keeps showing up that you didn't expect?
   - Where in your morning could you realistically do this — what has to happen before it, and what would you be trading it against?

3. Finding the Pattern in Your Own Data
   - Look at the last 30 days of your life. When did you feel genuinely good about your work or your day? Write 3-5 specific moments.
   - What do those moments have in common? Are you building something, analyzing something, connecting with someone, moving your body?
   - What decision have you been trying to 'figure out' through content consumption that your own daily experience might already be answering?

4. The Belief You're Carrying
   - Complete this sentence honestly: 'I consume self-improvement content because I believe ___.' Write the first thing that comes out.
   - What would you do with your time this week if you genuinely believed you already knew enough to take the next step?
   - Write one thing your past self — from 6 months ago — would call you out on right now, the way the blog's author was called out by his own journal entry.


Footer: "Reply with your answers — your reflections help others find their entry point."

Optional: Soft watercolor accent in corner.
