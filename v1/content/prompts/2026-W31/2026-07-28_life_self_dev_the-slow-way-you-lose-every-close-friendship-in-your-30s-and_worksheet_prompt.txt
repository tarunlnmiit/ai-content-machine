Create a reflective worksheet PDF (A4, portrait) for "The Friendship Fade Audit: Find Who You're Losing Before It's Final"

Layout:
- Header (top 15%): Soft gradient background. Title + subtitle about personal growth.
- 4 sections vertically stacked, each with:
  - Section title (warm orange or teal accent)
  - Reflective prompt in italics
  - Blank space for handwritten response (30-50% of section height)
  - Dividing line between sections

Colors: Warm cream background (#FFFBF0), teal accents (#0D9488), dark gray text

Sections:
1. Check Your Silent Ledger
   - Name three people you've quietly decided 'should reach out first.' What's the unspoken math you're running - why is it their turn and not yours?
   - When did you start treating your own outreach as spending and their outreach as earning? Try to date it, even roughly.
   - Which friendship are you currently explaining away with 'life's just hectic right now' when the truer story is that you stopped paying into it?
   - If you added up the balance on that ledger today, who owes whom - and does that framing even make sense once you say it out loud?

2. Find Your 'Next Sunday'
   - Do you have, or did you have, a standing ritual with someone - a weekly call, a lunch, a running joke? Name the person and the ritual.
   - Recall the first time you told them 'swamped this week, let's do it next [day]' instead of showing up. What was the actual reason, versus the excuse you gave?
   - What happened the second time it slipped - did either of you name it, or did the silence just get quietly accepted as normal?
   - Looking back, was there a single moment that ritual became 'interruptible'? What was happening in your life right then that made you treat it as optional?

3. Depth vs. Frequency Autopsy
   - Name one friendship you assumed could survive six months of silence because it was 'real.' Has it actually survived, or has it quietly expired?
   - Name one relationship that's stayed alive purely on frequency - a dumb voice note, a meme, a Tuesday text with no agenda. What's the small, low-effort thing that keeps it going?
   - Which friendships right now are you protecting with the 'depth alone will hold it' belief, and which one is most at risk of that belief being wrong?

4. Send the Specific-Memory Text
   - Pick the one person you suspect is in a slow fade right now. Write down one hyper-specific shared memory with them - not 'we should catch up,' but the exact bug, trip, joke, or moment only the two of you would recognize.
   - Draft the actual text you'd send: either that specific-memory opener, or the direct version - 'I feel like we've drifted, and I don't want to. Can we get on a call this week?' Which fits this person and why?
   - Name the real fear underneath not sending it - hearing 'yeah, we have,' hearing nothing back, or something else?
   - Pick a day this week you'll actually send it. Put it on the calendar right now.


Footer: "Reply with your answers — your reflections help others find their entry point."

Optional: Soft watercolor accent in corner.
