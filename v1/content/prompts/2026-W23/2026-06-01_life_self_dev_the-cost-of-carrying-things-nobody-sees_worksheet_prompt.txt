Create a reflective worksheet PDF (A4, portrait) for "Name the Weight You're Carrying"

Layout:
- Header (top 15%): Soft gradient background. Title + subtitle about personal growth.
- 4 sections vertically stacked, each with:
  - Section title (warm orange or teal accent)
  - Reflective prompt in italics
  - Blank space for handwritten response (30-50% of section height)
  - Dividing line between sections

Colors: Warm cream background (#FFFBF0), teal accents (#0D9488), dark gray text

Sections:
1. Find the Loop That's Running in the Background
   - Think of one unresolved thing you're still replaying (an argument, a loss, a decision). Write the exact moment it happened — where you were, what was said.
   - What would it look like for that thing to have an ending? Not resolution with the other person — just an ending you could write for yourself.
   - On a scale of 1-10, how much mental bandwidth is this loop taking up on an average day?
   - Name one task this week where you noticed the 'fog' — rereading the same paragraph, forgetting something small, forty minutes gone with nothing to show for it.

2. Trace Where the Weight Leaks Out
   - Think of someone close to you. Where has the half-second delay, the shortened patience, or the distance shown up with them recently?
   - Has anyone ever said something like 'you're here but not really here'? Write down what they said, as close to word-for-word as you remember.
   - What are you protecting people from by staying silent — and is that protection actually working, or is it costing the relationship something instead?
   - Pick one person you've been keeping at a slight remove. What's one true sentence you could say to them this week that isn't 'I'm fine'?

3. Do the Cost Accounting
   - List three things you've been carrying silently right now — no need to explain them, just name them.
   - For each one, estimate: how long have you been carrying it? A week, a year, longer?
   - What is the energy cost of containment versus the energy cost of naming it out loud to one person? Be honest about which is actually higher.
   - If your brain has a 'finite pool' of resources, what's one thing you'd get back — creatively, professionally, relationally — if this loop closed?

4. Choose One Small Act of Unloading
   - Pick the lightest of the three things from Section 3 — not the heaviest. Who is one safe person you could tell it to this week?
   - Draft the first sentence you'd use to say it out loud. Write it exactly as you'd say it.
   - What's the smallest possible version of 'not fine' you could admit to someone today?
   - Set a specific day and time this week to have that conversation. Put it somewhere you'll actually see it.


Footer: "Reply with your answers — your reflections help others find their entry point."

Optional: Soft watercolor accent in corner.
