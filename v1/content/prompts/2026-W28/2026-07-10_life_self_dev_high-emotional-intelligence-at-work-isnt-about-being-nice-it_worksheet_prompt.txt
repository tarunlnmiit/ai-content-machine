Create a reflective worksheet PDF (A4, portrait) for "The Ten-Second Rule: A Worksheet for Saying the Hard Thing"

Layout:
- Header (top 15%): Soft gradient background. Title + subtitle about personal growth.
- 4 sections vertically stacked, each with:
  - Section title (warm orange or teal accent)
  - Reflective prompt in italics
  - Blank space for handwritten response (30-50% of section height)
  - Dividing line between sections

Colors: Warm cream background (#FFFBF0), teal accents (#0D9488), dark gray text

Sections:
1. Find Your Silent Room
   - Think of a recent meeting or conversation where everyone nodded but the room felt tense underneath. What was the unspoken number, risk, or truth everyone could see but nobody named?
   - Whose exchanged glances or body language told you the real story that day?
   - What did staying quiet cost the team in the weeks after — a missed deadline, a redo, a blown launch?
   - Write the exact sentence you would have said if you'd had the nerve, the way the author said 'the real number is closer to eight.'

2. Check If It's the Junior Analyst Move
   - Is there someone on your team (or in your life) whose struggle you've been quietly fixing behind the scenes instead of naming directly?
   - What are you telling yourself about why silence is 'kinder' right now?
   - If this stayed unsaid for four more months, what's the blindsided moment that could follow — like the director pulling someone aside cold?
   - What would 'why did nobody tell me' sound like coming from this person, and does that change your urgency?

3. Draft the Sentence Using the Two Moves
   - Rewrite your hard truth using the shared-problem framing: swap 'you didn't' for 'we set this up wrong — can we look at what happened.'
   - If the issue is someone avoiding an obvious problem, write the plain-and-owned version: 'I'm going to say the thing nobody's saying, and I might be wrong: ___.'
   - Read both versions out loud. Which one still sounds true if you're wrong about the details?
   - Type the final sentence into your notes app right now — not rehearsed in your head, actually written down.

4. Run the Ego Gut-Check
   - Ask yourself: would I still want this said if I later found out I misread the situation?
   - Is any part of this about needing to be right, needing to win, or needing credit for catching it?
   - Describe the quiet, reluctant feeling versus the loud, urgent feeling — which one are you actually feeling about this specific thing?
   - Name one situation from the past year where you now realize you were chasing being right instead of being useful.


Footer: "Reply with your answers — your reflections help others find their entry point."

Optional: Soft watercolor accent in corner.
