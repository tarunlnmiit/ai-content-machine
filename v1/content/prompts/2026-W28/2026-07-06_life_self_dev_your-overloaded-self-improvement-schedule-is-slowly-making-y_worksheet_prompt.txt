Create a reflective worksheet PDF (A4, portrait) for "The Slot-11 Audit: Find Who Your Schedule Is Actually Building"

Layout:
- Header (top 15%): Soft gradient background. Title + subtitle about personal growth.
- 4 sections vertically stacked, each with:
  - Section title (warm orange or teal accent)
  - Reflective prompt in italics
  - Blank space for handwritten response (30-50% of section height)
  - Dividing line between sections

Colors: Warm cream background (#FFFBF0), teal accents (#0D9488), dark gray text

Sections:
1. Count Your Habits Like He Counted His
   - List every tracked habit you currently run — app, spreadsheet, streak, all of it. How many total?
   - Which ones would you defend as 'the ones that matter most'? Circle no more than 4.
   - For everything not circled: when did you last do it without checking a box first — could you even tell?
   - Which habit on your list looks most virtuous on paper (like his tracking spreadsheet) but you haven't examined its actual effect?

2. Find Your Own 'Mom Called at 7:40' Moment
   - Think of the last time a routine or tracker made you irritated at a person you love for interrupting it. What was the routine?
   - What did you choose that day — the checkbox or the person? Be specific about what you said or did.
   - Is there a closed door in your life right now — a partner, kid, friend — who's stopped arguing and just says 'okay'?
   - Write the sentence you'd say out loud if someone asked you to skip your routine tonight. Does it sound like declining a meeting?

3. Find What's Quietly Starving
   - Like his fiction reading, name one thing you used to enjoy that produces no loggable number — has it quietly stopped?
   - Do you feel low-grade guilt during hours that don't 'feed a cell' in some tracker? Name the last hour that happened.
   - What's your version of his Sunday cricket reruns — time you've labeled 'wasted' and are one productivity podcast away from cutting?
   - If you cut that 'dead time,' what filled it instead — and was that actually an improvement?

4. Run the Cut-to-4 Experiment
   - Pick your 4 keepers, the way he kept one workout, one 5-minute journal, 30 minutes of writing, and sleep by 11.
   - Write down exactly what you're dropping this week — name each one, not just 'the extras.'
   - Estimate hours reclaimed per week, like his 12 hours. Where specifically will that time go — a person, a hobby, sleep?
   - Set a 3-week check-in date (his output tripled by week 3) — what's the one honest metric you'll check, besides a streak count?


Footer: "Reply with your answers — your reflections help others find their entry point."

Optional: Soft watercolor accent in corner.
