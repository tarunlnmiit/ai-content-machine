## 4-Beat Script

### BEAT 1 — HOOK (0–4s)
- **VO:** "I tracked fourteen habits. My consistency score was ninety-one percent. And my wife still closed the door on me."
- **On-screen (burn-in):** `91% consistent. Door still closed.`
- **Cue:** Face to camera, quiet, hard cut in.

### BEAT 2 — PROBLEM + REFRAME (4–10s)
- **VO:** "Every guru diagnoses the same disease: you're inconsistent, you need a tighter system. They have cause and effect backwards. The schedule wasn't failing. It was succeeding — at optimizing me into a metric."
- **On-screen:** `The schedule was the damage. Not the gaps in it.`
- **Cue:** B-roll: overloaded Notion tracker, 14 streaks glowing.

### BEAT 3 — VALUE / PROOF (10–22s) — *the framework is the proof*
- **VO:** "So I did the one thing the advice never asks: I looked at the data honestly. The weeks I scored highest were the weeks I wrote nothing worth reading and called my parents least. I cut fourteen habits to four. Mornings went from two and a half hours to forty-five minutes — twelve hours a week back. Within three weeks my writing output tripled. Not a better system. The four that mattered finally had room."
- **On-screen (stat build):** `14 → 4 habits` · `+12 hrs/week` · `writing ×3` · `the 12 I dropped were suffocating the 4 that mattered`
- **Cue:** Clean kinetic-type stat cards, warm tone (no screen demo — this is the reframe landing).

### BEAT 4 — PAYOFF + CTA (22–32s)
- **VO (payoff):** "So here's the test, no app required: don't ask 'am I consistent?' Ask 'who is this consistency producing?' If it's someone your family talks to through a closed door — the problem was never that you weren't doing enough."
- **On-screen:** `Ask: who is my consistency producing?`

## Caption (essay-style for a heavy topic; side-notes live here)

My wife asked if I wanted to watch a movie. I heard myself say "I can't, I have my deep work block, then journaling, then wind-down" — and pulled up my Notion tracker to prove it. She said "okay" and closed the door. I had 14 active streaks. I'd chosen a checkbox over the person I built the whole "better life" for.

That's what self-improvement burnout actually looks like. Not exhaustion. Not quitting. It looks like winning, on paper, while the person the plan was for slowly disappears.

I cut 14 habits to 4. Reclaimed ~12 hours a week. My writing tripled in three weeks — because the habits that mattered finally had room instead of running on fumes at slot 11 of 14.

The test I'd offer you tonight: not "am I consistent?" but "who is this consistency producing?"
