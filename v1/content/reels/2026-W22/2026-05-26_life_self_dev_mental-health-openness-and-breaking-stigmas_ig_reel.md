## Caption (essay-style — Life emotional piece; side-notes live here, not in the VO)

Break a bone and people sign your cast. Say you haven't slept in three weeks because your chest feels like it's caving in — and the room goes quiet. Or worse, you get "have you tried going outside more?"

40% of men won't talk about their mental health. Not to a friend, not to family, not to anyone (Priory Group research). It's not weakness. It's a script we inherited — "real men handle it" — old code still running on new hardware.

A friend called me at midnight once. Flat voice, long pauses, every answer functional instead of alive. And I went straight into fix-it mode: budgeting tools, resume tweaks, side income — treating his collapse like a logistics problem. He went quiet and said, "Yeah… I know all that." That's when it clicked. He hadn't called because he lacked solutions. He called because he was alone inside the pressure and needed someone to sit in it with him. So I stopped. I just said, "That sounds really heavy, man." And it changed — not because the problem left, because he stopped feeling managed and started feeling heard.

Here's the part that stays with me: nobody mocks you for hiring a personal trainer to get stronger. Say "therapist" and suddenly you're "not strong enough to handle your own life." Same logic. Opposite reaction. Some moments don't need a mechanic. They need a witness.

The strength we inherited — the kind built on silence — is just brittleness waiting for enough pressure. Then it shatters. The other kind bends. Vulnerability isn't the opposite of strength. It's proof of it. And every generation that stays quiet just passes the bill forward.
