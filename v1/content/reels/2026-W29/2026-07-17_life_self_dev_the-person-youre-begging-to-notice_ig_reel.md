## 5-Beat Script

### BEAT 1 — HOOK (0–5s)
- **VO:** "I counted one chat thread. Forty-one messages from me. Nine from him. Six were a thumbs-up."
- **On-screen (burn-in):** `41 from me · 9 from him · 6 = 👍`
- **Cue:** Face to camera, quiet, hard cut in. No greeting. Re-record 5×, keep the flattest, least-performed take (the calm delivery *is* the hook here).

### BEAT 2 — PROBLEM (5–15s)
- **VO:** "I read data for a living. Ten years. But I'd never run the numbers on my own friendships. So I opened another thread — a friend had asked how my week went. Twice. Then stopped."
- **On-screen:** `Never once ran the numbers on the people who stayed.`
- **Cue:** Slow push-in on face; single hard cut on "Twice." Let the silence sit a half-beat.

### BEAT 3 — REVEAL + PROOF (15–35s) — *the mechanism is the proof*
- **VO:** "Here's the mechanism. Your brain doesn't want the unavailable person — it wants the unresolved ending. So it prices their silence high, and the steady people at nothing. Your nervous system reads steady as nothing happening. I wasn't unlucky in who I chose. I was measuring intensity when I should've been measuring consistency."
- **On-screen (idea build, one phrase per sub-beat):** `unavailable = the unresolved ending` · `steady = read as "nothing happening"` · `you measure INTENSITY — care is CONSISTENCY`
- **Cue:** Clean kinetic-type cards over a held face shot, warm tone. No demo, no stock — the reframe landing is the proof (08 §value = the framework for a mindset piece). Cut hard between the three phrases.

### BEAT 4 — PAYOFF (35–47s) — *the [QUOTABLE]*

- **On-screen:** `6 months on his silence. 0 minutes on the one who showed up.`
- **Cue:** Back to a still, direct face-to-camera. This is the line people screenshot — hold it, don't rush the delivery.

### BEAT 5 — CTA (47–55s)

## Caption (essay-style — Life emotional piece; side-notes live here, not in the VO)

I do this for a living. Ten years of looking at data and asking what it's actually saying instead of what I want it to say. And I had never once run the numbers on my own friendships.

Forty-one messages from me. Nine from him. Six of those nine were a thumbs-up.

Here's the part nobody says out loud: the pain was never really about the person ignoring me. Your brain doesn't want them — it wants the unresolved ending. The question mark. So it prices their silence high and the steady people at nothing. Real care is low-variance. Quiet. And a nervous system tuned to notice *change* reads steady as nothing happening.

The tell isn't warmth in someone's language. It's whether they were there when it was inconvenient. Words are cheap; actions are the only real data.

I'd spent six months auditing one person's silence for meaning, and zero minutes noticing the friend who showed up for every single thing without ever being asked. That's the actual bill — not the chasing, the friendship you let quietly starve while you were busy.

You don't have to delete anyone. You just move where your emotional energy goes.
