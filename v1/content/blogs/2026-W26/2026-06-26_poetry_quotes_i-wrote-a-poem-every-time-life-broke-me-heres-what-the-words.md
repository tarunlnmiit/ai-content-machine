# The Room No One Entered

This one came from a quiet Tuesday — the kind where everything went right and something still felt wrong. It's about the particular loneliness of being good at showing up.

![The Room No One Entered](/content/blogs/2026-W26/2026-06-26_poetry_quotes_i-wrote-a-poem-every-time-life-broke-me-heres-what-the-words_images/01_context_quiet-empty-room-dusty-light.jpg)
*The Room No One Entered — Photo by [Arman](https://www.pexels.com/photo/empty-chairs-in-an-abandoned-church-27549612/) on Pexels*

> I pass the salt before they ask.
> I laugh at the right moment.
> I know which mug is whose.
> 
> At 11pm I wash the dishes
> and somewhere in the soap and steam
> I forget what I wanted
> to say ten years ago.
> 
> There's a version of me
> still mid-sentence
> in a room no one entered.
> 
> I smile. I respond. I arrive on time.
> The days clock in and out without me.
> 
> Nobody notices
> because I am very good at this —
> being present
> like furniture is present,
> useful, in place,
> not missing anything
> anyone can name.

There's a specific ache in being reliably present while feeling absent from your own life — and most of us have lived inside it without having the words. If this found you somewhere mid-sentence, I write more like this — subscribe below.
