1
Check Your Silent Ledger

Name three people you've quietly decided "should reach out first." What's the unspoken math — why their turn, not yours?

When did you start treating your outreach as spending and theirs as earning? Date it, even roughly.

Which friendship are you explaining away with "life's just hectic" when the truer story is you stopped paying into it?

Add up the balance today — who owes whom? Does that framing even survive being said out loud?

Who's on the ledger
2
Find Your "Next Sunday"

Name a standing ritual you have or had — a weekly call, a lunch, a running joke — and who it was with.

Recall the first "swamped this week, let's do it next [day]". What was the real reason, versus the excuse?

What happened the second time it slipped — did either of you name it, or did the silence just get accepted?

Pinpoint the moment it became interruptible. What was happening in your life right then?

The ritual that slipped
3
Depth vs. Frequency Autopsy

Name one friendship you assumed could survive six months of silence because it was "real." Has it survived — or quietly expired?

Name one relationship kept alive by pure frequency — a dumb voice note, a meme, a Tuesday text. What's the small habit that does it?

Which friendship are you protecting on "depth alone will hold it" — and which one is most at risk of that belief being wrong?

Depth vs. frequency tally
4
Send the Specific-Memory Text

Pick the one person you suspect is in a slow fade right now.

Write the hyper-specific memory only the two of you would recognize — not "we should catch up," the exact bug, trip, or joke.

Draft the actual text: the memory opener, or the direct line — "I feel like we've drifted, and I don't want to. Can we get on a call this week?" Which fits, and why?

Name the real fear under not sending it. Then pick the day this week you'll send it — right now.
