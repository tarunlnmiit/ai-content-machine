What Interviewers Actually Write Down

Five exercises to build the judgment stories interviewers score — before you walk into the next room.

1
Write Your Own 'Why Not' Line

Name one project where you picked a model, metric, or method.

Write the alternative you rejected — not just what you chose.

Finish: "I chose X over Y" because ___.

No alternative comes to mind? That's the gap they'll flag.

Your tradeoff line
2
Find Your Leakage Story

Recall a result that looked too good, or a mistake caught before it shipped.

Sentence 1: what looked wrong.

Sentence 2: how you caught it.

Sentence 3: what you did next.

Your 3-sentence story
3
Business-Cost the Metric

Pick a project where you optimized accuracy, AUC, RMSE, or similar.

Write what that metric cost or saved — dollars, time, users.

Blank? Go find the number before your next interview.

Metric → business cost
4
Draft Your One Real Question

Write one question about the team's actual work — not perks.

Model: "What's the messiest dataset you're fighting right now?"

Or: "Where does this model actually break in production?"

Say it out loud once before the interview.

Your real question
5
Pressure-Test an Underspecified Prompt

Take a vague ask: "build a model to predict churn."

Before touching code, write the clarifying question you'd ask first.

What decision is this number actually feeding?

Compare to your instinct to just start building. That gap is what gets scored.
