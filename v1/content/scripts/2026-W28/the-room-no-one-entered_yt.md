There's a version of me

I smile. I respond. I arrive on time.
The days clock in and out without me.

Nobody notices
because I am very good at this —
being present
like furniture is present,
useful, in place,
not missing anything

Here's what this poem is actually about, and I don't mean the meaning of it — I mean what it does. It's about the particular loneliness of being good at showing up. Not the loud kind of lonely. The functional kind. You pass the salt. You know which mug is whose. You are, by every visible measure, there. And yet there's a sentence you started years ago that you never got to finish, and you've gotten so practiced at being useful that nobody — including you — notices it's still hanging in the air.

[PAUSE]

That line, "being present like furniture is present" — that's the one I keep coming back to. Furniture is reliable. Furniture is in place. Nobody worries about the chair. And somewhere along the way that can start to describe a person. Useful. In place. Not missing anything anyone can name.

[PERSONAL_INSERT: I've spent whole stretches of my life being the person who remembers everyone's coffee order and never once says the thing he actually came to say. And the scary part wasn't sadness. It was how smooth it felt. How easy it is to keep the surface running while some part of you stays mid-sentence in a room, waiting for someone to walk in and ask what you were about to say.]

[PALMIER_BG: the empty chair again, held long and still, room slowly darkening]

So if this found you somewhere mid-sentence — that ache of being reliably present while feeling absent from your own life — you're not broken, and you're not the only one. Most of us have lived inside this without ever having the words for it. That's what the poem is for. To be enough that you noticed. And that is enough.

[PAUSE]
