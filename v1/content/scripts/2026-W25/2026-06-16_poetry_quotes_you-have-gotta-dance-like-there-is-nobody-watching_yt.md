[ANIMATION: 3-second title card — "You Have to Dance Like Nobody's Watching"]

There's a video I shot last year. Six seconds. Me, alone in my apartment at eleven at night, doing something embarrassing enough that I almost deleted it before anyone could see.

I didn't delete it.

It became the most me thing I've ever put on the internet. And most days, I forget how close I came to erasing it.

We all have a move we keep holding back. This poem is about that move.

[BROLL: empty room at night, window light, someone moving alone]

There's a version of yourself that exists only when nobody's watching. Not a better version. An unfiltered one. The one that moves before the movement is polished.

I've been making content for three years. The pieces that connected — the ones where people replied with "this is exactly me" — were almost always the ones where I forgot to perform.

[PERSONAL_INSERT: One example was a poem I wrote during a period when I was trying to make sense of things I couldn't neatly explain. There was no framework behind it. No content strategy. No lesson. Just a feeling I needed to put somewhere.
I almost didn't share it because it felt unfinished. Too personal. Too specific to me.
But the responses weren't about the craft of the poem. People weren't dissecting the structure or the language. They were telling me about their own lives. Their own grief. Their own confusion. Their own version of the feeling I thought belonged only to me.
What connected wasn't polish. It was recognition.
I wasn't trying to sound wise. I was trying to be honest. And somehow that reached further than the pieces where I spent hours trying to get everything exactly right.]

The word for this is unselfconsciousness. It's the hardest creative skill I know — not because it requires talent, but because it requires forgetting to care. And our brains are engineered to care.

[ANIMATION: 3-second lower third — "Not for the encore. For the ache."]

[BROLL: hands writing in a notebook, low light, unhurried]

Here's what nobody tells you about dancing like nobody's watching: eventually, people watch.

And the watching changes you. You start pre-editing. You begin performing the version of yourself who once moved freely. The authentic act creates an audience. The audience creates a mirror. The mirror creates self-consciousness.

[PAUSE]

Suddenly the dance is choreographed — and it wasn't supposed to be.

What I've found is a small practice: one thing a week with no audience at all. Not for a post, not as a draft, not to revisit later. Write a poem nobody sees. Build something to see if I can. Move before I know where I'm going.

The unobserved moments keep your center of gravity low. They remind you what it feels like to begin without asking permission. And when the observed moments come — the posts, the things you send into the world — they carry some residue of that. You can feel the difference. Your audience can too.

[BROLL: person stepping toward a window, light across their face]

The poem isn't about dancing.

It's about the draft still sitting in Notes. The creator who keeps refining until the moment passes. The person who knows what they want to say — but is waiting for the room to clear first.

[PAUSE]

The room won't clear.

There's no applause coming in advance. There's no version of this where you start after the self-consciousness disappears — because the self-consciousness only disappears inside the doing.

You already know the move. You've known it for a while.

Dance anyway.
