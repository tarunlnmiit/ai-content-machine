# I Stopped Googling Travel Guides. I Interrogated an AI Instead.

**Hook:** Every AI travel recommendation sounds the same — same 10 things, same tourist loop. The model isn't broken. Your prompt is.

## The Problem With How Everyone Prompts AI for Travel

Ask any AI "best things to do in Paris" and you'll get the Eiffel Tower, the Louvre, and eight other items from every listicle published since 2015.

[BROLL: Screen recording — generic AI chat showing "top 10 Paris" results]

That's not the model failing you. That's you prompting it like a search engine instead of a researcher. The output quality is a direct reflection of the job description you gave it — and "best things to do" is the vaguest job description imaginable.

## The Five-Part Prompt That Changed Everything

I gave the AI a structured job instead of a vague question. Five components:

**Role** — historian, not chatbot. Not a travel influencer, not a trip advisor. A historian who knows context, origin, and obscurity.

**Task** — three lesser-known sites, with the era and the address. Specific deliverable, not an open-ended brainstorm.

[BROLL: Prompt structure written out on screen — 5 numbered components]

**Exclude** — no "I filter," no top-10 lists. Explicitly rule out the tourist loop before it starts.

**Format** — a table, not a wall of text. Structure forces precision. A table can't hide vague answers.

**Verify** — two sources per recommendation, flag conflicts, no fabrication. You're asking it to show its work.
