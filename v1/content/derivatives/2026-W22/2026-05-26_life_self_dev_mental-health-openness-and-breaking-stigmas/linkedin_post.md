# LinkedIn Post — Life / Mental Health

---

Nobody mocks you for hiring a personal trainer to get physically stronger.

Mention a therapist and suddenly you're "not strong enough to handle your own life."

Same logic. Same structure. Completely different social response.

That inconsistency doesn't happen randomly. It's inherited — passed down through workplaces and living rooms until it became invisible. "Real men don't cry." "Therapy is for people who can't cope." These scripts got reinforced until nobody noticed them anymore.

But the cost isn't invisible. Globally, men die by suicide at significantly higher rates than women. That's the downstream result of a long chain of "I'm fine."

I spent years white-knuckling it. The kind of exhaustion that isn't physical. Passive-aggressive in meetings. Rude to people I cared about. Waking at 3 AM running mental loops that went nowhere. I told myself that was just ambition.

It wasn't.

First therapy session, I was asked: "What would you say to a friend going through what you're going through?"

The answer came fast. You don't have to carry this alone. You deserve support.

Then the obvious question: why wouldn't I say that to myself?

The version of strength worth building doesn't require silence. It hires a therapist for the same reason it hires a trainer — because getting stronger requires outside pressure and honest feedback. It asks "how are you?" and actually means it.
