Nobody mocks you for hiring a personal trainer. The moment you mention a therapist, you're suddenly "not strong enough to handle your own life."

Same logic. Same structure. Completely different social response.

Here's the mechanism nobody names: we have a clear social script for physical pain and none for emotional pain. Break a bone and people sign the cast, hold the door, bring food. Tell someone your chest has been caving in for three weeks and the room goes quiet — or you get "have you tried going outside more?"

So we improvise the missing script with dismissal, logic, and "just push through it." We hand someone drowning a map of the shoreline instead of a rope.

I did the same thing to a friend who called late one night sounding off. I went straight into optimization mode — budgeting tools, side-income ideas, resume tweaks. Treated his emotional collapse like a logistics problem waiting for a cleaner spreadsheet. He went quiet and said, almost under his breath, "Yeah... I know all that."

That was the click. He hadn't called because he lacked solutions. He called because he was alone inside the pressure and needed another human to sit in it with him for a minute.

I stopped mid-sentence. No more fixes. I just said, "That sounds really heavy, man." The conversation changed immediately — not because the problem disappeared, but because he finally felt heard instead of managed.

The useful rule: presence before fixes. When someone opens up, don't engineer the pain away. Name the weight first. The solutions can wait — and often they were never what was being asked for.
