The pieces that resonate most are almost always the ones I nearly deleted.

Three years into making content, I've noticed a pattern I can't explain away.

The essays that get the most replies — where people say 'this is exactly me' — weren't the ones I spent the most time polishing. They were the mid-thought ideas. The rough drafts I almost didn't post. The 11pm six-second videos I nearly erased before anyone saw.

The word for this is unselfconsciousness. And it's the hardest creative skill I know. Not because it requires talent. Because it requires forgetting to care — and our brains are built to care.

There's a paradox here though: the authentic act creates an audience. The audience creates a mirror. The mirror makes you self-conscious. Suddenly you're performing the version of yourself who once moved freely.

What I've found is a small practice: one thing per week with no audience at all. Not for a post, not as a draft. Write something nobody sees. Move before you know where you're going.

The unobserved moments keep your center of gravity low. And when the observed moments come, people can feel the difference.
