Subject: The second-order game most people never find
Preview: It's not about adding more habits. It's about improving how you improve.

Most self-improvement advice is a treadmill. You read the book, start the habit, fall off, repeat. What nobody explains is that there's a second-order game running underneath — one where getting better at *getting better* is the actual unlock.

In this week's piece, I broke down 5 recursive loops that change the structure of improvement, not just the tactics.

The one that surprised me most while writing it: Loop 3. I looked back at months of journal entries expecting to see patterns in what I was working on. What I found instead was a pattern in where I was consistently wrong about myself — wrong about what generated energy, wrong about what I was avoiding, wrong about cause and effect in my own behavior. That kind of second-order review is completely different from ordinary journaling.

Loop 5 is the deepest. Behavior follows identity. If your self-concept doesn't update as you improve, you'll unconsciously self-sabotage back to whoever you used to be. Most people experience this without a name for it.
