Waking Up to
What We've Built

There's a moment — usually quiet, usually late — when the performance stops making sense.

The Poem

Waking up to a realization
About how the world is changing
Thoughts driven by exteriority
No care about what lies within
People drooling over aesthetics
Shitting all over the hearts of the good ones
Praising the false gods
Demeaning deserving humans
Talking about love and life
Not knowing the true meaning
How our civilization has turned out
Wearing layers of masks themselves
Ready to tear down the true faces
Only to make themselves happy
Only to make themselves happy

What this does to you

The good ones aren't naive.
They just won't trade their interior for applause.

The internet says it wants authenticity. The numbers often tell a different story.

The performative version spreads — not because it's truer, but because it's easier to consume.

Every creator faces the same fork: build a body of work, or wear a costume. The costume grows faster. The work lasts longer.

The mask paradox

Confidence doesn't need to flatten anything. Defence does.

The mask doesn't just hide — it needs to. Authenticity is an indictment of performance. You can't have both in the same room and pretend they're equal.

"Ready to tear down the true faces — not by accident. Ready."

Not righteousness. Not values.
Just hunger — wearing borrowed language about love and life.

The one thing to carry

The poem doesn't offer a fix.
It offers a name.

1
Name what's happening
Naming ends confusion — the confusion of thinking you're the problem when you won't wear the mask.
2
Refuse to disappear
You're not naive for being real. You're visible — in a world that's learned to find visibility threatening.
3
Stop being fooled
Waking up isn't despair. It's the end of being fooled. That's enough to begin with.
