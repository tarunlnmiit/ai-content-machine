The Problem
Most people type this:
"Best things to do in Paris?"
And they get the Eiffel Tower. The Louvre. Sacré-Cœur. The same 10 things on every listicle since 2009.
The model isn't failing you.
The prompt is.
LLMs are prediction engines — vague input returns the average answer. You get the median tourist experience. A postcard.

Steps 1 + 2
Role → Task
Step 1 — Assign a Role
"You are an expert architectural historian and Paris travel guide with decades of experience uncovering overlooked historical sites... Think like a local historian who also loves storytelling."
↳ Shifts response from "average tourist answer" to what an obsessive Parisian historian would say. The persona filters every word.
Step 2 — Define Task Precisely
"Identify three lesser-known sites — palaces, mansions, small chapels... include arrondissement, architectural style, era of construction, one unique feature..."
↳ Every constraint is a guardrail. Prevents defaulting to the obvious.

Steps 3 + 4
Exclude → Format
Step 3 — Exclude Explicitly
"Do not include Notre-Dame, the Eiffel Tower, Sacré-Cœur, the Louvre, or Musée d'Orsay. Exclude any site in top-10 tourist lists or heavily photographed on social media."
↳ Like telling a sommelier: "No Bordeaux. Surprise me." Forces higher-quality, lower-probability outputs.
Step 4 — Dictate Output Format
"Return a Markdown table: Name | Location (arrondissement) | Style/Era | Key Feature | Historical Anecdote | Why unique."
↳ You're giving AI a form to fill out, not a blank page to wander. Reduces hallucination, increases density.

Step 5 + Principles
Verify. Then think like a contractor.
Step 5 — Demand Verification
"Verify each site against ≥2 reliable sources (Wikipedia, official heritage sites). Provide links. If sources conflict, note discrepancies."
↳ Activates source-anchored generation. Doesn't eliminate hallucinations — makes them far easier to catch.
4 Rules to Prompt Like a Pro
1
Role first — it sets the entire tone
2
Structure the output (tables > prose)
3
Exclude explicitly — as important as what TO do
4
Iterate without ego — refine the prompt, not expectations
