Breath of Poetry

You have to dance like nobody's watching.

Even after they start.
"There's a move you've been holding back.
This poem is about that move."
the confession

I almost deleted it.

There's a video I shot last year. Six seconds. Me, alone in my apartment at 11pm, doing something embarrassing enough that I nearly erased it before anyone could see.
"It became the most me thing I've ever put on the internet."
Most days, I forget how close I came to erasing it.
We all have a move we keep holding back.
the poem
You have to dance
when the playlist ends
when the room empties
when no one is left to grade your form.
You have to move
even with the wrong body
the untrained hands
the borrowed rhythm.
Most of us wait
for permission we never get —
for applause that is always
one brave act away.
Dance anyway.
Not for the encore.
For the ache in your chest
that finally has somewhere to go.
the insight

The unfiltered pieces are the ones that land.

Three years of content. The pieces that hit — the ones people replied to with "this is exactly me" — were almost always the ones where I forgot to perform.
  • The six-second video I almost deleted
  • The blog draft that felt too personal, too unfinished
  • The poem with no framework, no lesson — only feeling
What connected wasn't polish.
It was recognition.
the paradox

Eventually, people watch.

Here's what nobody tells you about dancing like nobody's watching:
  • Authentic act → creates an audience
  • Audience → creates a mirror
  • Mirror → creates self-consciousness
  • Suddenly the dance is choreographed
You begin performing the version of yourself who once moved freely.
I don't have a clean resolution to this.
the practice

One unobserved thing, every week.

The small practice that keeps your center of gravity low:
  1. 1
    Write a poem nobody sees. Not a draft — for no one.
  2. 2
    Build something to see if you can. No post planned.
  3. 3
    Move before you know where you're going. Begin without asking permission.
The unobserved keeps your making honest.
Your audience feels the difference.
