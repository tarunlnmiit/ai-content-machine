7 Simple Habits That Quietly Rewired My Productivity

The exhaustion of chasing perfect systems — and the quiet relief of returning to habits honest enough to survive your worst days.

@mistakenlyhuman
The Problem

Why Productivity Systems Fail

  • They require a perfect structure across an imperfect life
  • Complexity collapses on hard days
  • The fix: habits simple enough to survive your worst day
@mistakenlyhuman
Habit 1

The Shutdown Ritual

  • Four minutes: scan tasks, note what's open, write one line on what got done
  • Say out loud: "Shutdown complete"
  • Gives your nervous system a real closure signal — brains fixate on open loops until you do
@mistakenlyhuman
Habit 2

The Weekly Brain Dump

  • 20 minutes, once a week — write everything in your head
  • No organization, no categories, just out
  • Offloading frees cognitive resources for actual thinking
@mistakenlyhuman
Habit 3

Eat One Frog

  • Identify your most dreaded task the night before
  • Do it first — and only one
  • Avoidance runs in the background like an open program; one frog closes it
@mistakenlyhuman
Habit 4

The Done List

  • At day's end, write the 5 most meaningful things you did
  • Not everything — what moved something
  • Corrects your estimate of your own capacity over time
@mistakenlyhuman
Habit 5

One 90-Minute Deep Work Block

  • First 15–20 minutes are on-ramp; short blocks end right as you hit stride
  • Each context switch costs 15–20 minutes of recovery time
  • One protected block beats four fragmented Pomodoros for output quality
@mistakenlyhuman
Habit 6

Write Tomorrow's Top 3 Tonight

  • Three priorities, written the night before — three forces a choice, five doesn't
  • Night-before planning uses full context; morning planning uses depleted decision capacity
  • Morning-you should never decide what to work on — past-you already did
@mistakenlyhuman
Habit 7

Track One Metric Per Week

  • One number — whatever matters most this week
  • Tracking ten things creates measurement overhead that becomes its own procrastination
  • What gets measured gets noticed. Noticing is most of the work.
@mistakenlyhuman
