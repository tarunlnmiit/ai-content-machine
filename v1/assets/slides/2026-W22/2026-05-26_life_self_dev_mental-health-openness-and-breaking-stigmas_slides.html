The Lie We Inherited About Strength

BREAK A BONE.

PEOPLE SHOW UP.

"Say you're drowning.
The room goes quiet."

40%
The numbers don't lie
—

40% of men won't talk about their mental health —
not to friends, not to family, not to anyone.

—

Globally, men die by suicide at significantly
higher rates than women.

—

This isn't weakness. It's a systemic failure
to give people permission to struggle out loud.

02
Inherited, not chosen

The stigma is
inherited, not chosen

—

"Real men don't cry."

—

"Therapy is for people who can't handle life."

—

These scripts ran so long they became wallpaper —
nobody notices them, but they're still running.

03
Where we go wrong

We confuse support
with problem-solving

—

Someone calls because they need to be heard.

—

We reach for budgeting tools, action plans, and logic.

Some moments don't need a mechanic.
They need a witness.

04
The double standard

The personal trainer
double standard

—

No stigma: hiring a personal trainer
to get physically stronger.

—

Automatic stigma: hiring a therapist
to get emotionally stronger.

—

Same logic. Completely different
social response.

05
What strength actually is

Two kinds of strength

—

Strength built on suppression looks solid —
until the right pressure hits.

—

Strength built on honesty bends — but doesn't break.

Vulnerability isn't the opposite of strength.
It's proof of it.

06
The practice

What breaking silence
actually looks like

—

Ask how someone is —
and actually wait for the real answer.

—

Listen without immediately pivoting to solutions.

—

Mention therapy without treating it like
a last resort or a verdict.

07
A final word

The wall cracks one
conversation at a time

—

You don't have to carry your struggle alone.

—

Neither does the person sitting next to you.

Start there.
