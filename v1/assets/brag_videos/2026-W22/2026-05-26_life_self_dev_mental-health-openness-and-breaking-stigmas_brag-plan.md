# Brag Plan: "The Casserole" (mental-health openness, published in An Injustice!)

## What is this?
Not an app — a personal essay about inherited mental-health stigma: the double standard
where a broken bone earns a casserole and a signed cast, but a breakdown earns "have you
tried going outside." It was published in the Medium publication **An Injustice!** and
retitled "The Silent Killer No One Talks About (And It's Not What You Think)." The brag is
real: real writing, real pickup, live URL.

## The angle
A quiet argument delivered as writing, not a product demo. The video opens on the double
standard stated flat — break a bone, get a casserole; break down, get "try going outside" —
grounds it in a real statistic (40% of men won't talk about their mental health), turns on
the essay's central image (a drowning person handed a map instead of a rope), proves it ran
in a real publication, and closes on the essay's thesis. Specific to this piece: its actual
lines, its actual warm photography, and social proof that is a live headline, not a stat.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
The double standard as a two-line contrast card, both lines landing together by ~1.5s over a
dimmed bench photo: **"Break a bone. Get a casserole."** (off-white) then **"Break down. Get
'try going outside.'"** (muted). The contrast IS the hook — it must land clean and fast.

## Key moments (the middle)
- The scale of it, grounded in real research: **"40% of men won't talk about their mental
  health. Not to friends. Not to family."** (Priory Group research, from the essay.)
- The essay's central image as an amber Playfair pull-quote: *"We hand someone drowning a map
  of the shoreline instead of a rope."* — the argumentative peak.
- The proof: a restrained browser mockup of the live An Injustice! article — the real retitled
  headline and the real URL — as social proof that the argument landed somewhere real.

## Outro / punchline
The essay's thesis, held clean, the key word in sage: **"Vulnerability isn't the opposite of
strength. It's proof of it."**

## Tone
- Preset: default (clean, postable; contrast / pattern-interrupt pacing)
- Interpretation: fast clean hook, then longer holds on the ache and the proof. Large still
  type, restraint over energy — the subject is mental health and earns attention by conviction.

## Format: vertical — 1080x1920
## Duration: ~21.5s (reading-time floors extended each scene past the 3-4s storyboard targets)

## Visual identity (from the Life niche look bible)
- Background `#1a1208` (warm deep brown), card bg `#231a0e`
- Text `#fdf8f0` (warm off-white), muted labels `#a0856a`
- Accent `#f59e0b` (amber); resolution accent `#86efac` (sage) — reserved for the closing line
- Display font Playfair Display (700 headline, 400 italic pull-quote); body font Inter (400/500/600)
- Warm amber cast + film grain + vignette throughout (the niche's atmospheric constants); the
  essay's own warm photography (bench, window, close conversation) as full-bleed backdrops.

## Audio direction
Warm, sparse intimate bed. Source: local Life-niche BGM ("I Just Want Quiet — Slow Acoustic"),
trimmed from an 80s offset to the exact 21.5s duration, volume 0.20, 1.2s fade in / 1.8s fade
out. Enters low under the hook, holds flat and quiet, fades to near-silence under the closing
line so the words land last. No swell, no drops, no percussive energy — restraint is the point.
