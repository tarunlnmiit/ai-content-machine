# Brag Plan: "The Lie We Inherited About Strength" (published in An Injustice!)

## What is this app?
Not an app — a personal essay about inherited mental-health stigma in men: the double
standard where physical injury earns support and emotional pain earns "have you tried
going outside." It was published in the Medium publication An Injustice! (2026-06-19),
retitled "The Silent Killer No One Talks About." The brag is: real writing, real pickup.

## The angle
The premise is a quiet argument delivered as a piece of writing, not a product demo. The
video walks the essay's own turn — from the inherited lie ("Real men don't cry") to the
line that flips it (you'll hire a personal trainer for your body but stigmatize hiring a
therapist for your mind — same logic, opposite response) — and lands on the fact that a
real publication ran it. Specific to this piece, not generic: it uses the essay's actual
lines and the actual warm photography, and its social proof is a live headline, not a stat.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
The inherited lie, stated flat and large: **"Real men don't cry."** in Playfair Display
over a warm window-light photo (person by a window). No motion tricks — the line holds long
enough to sting, then the video spends 18 seconds dismantling it.

## Key moments (the middle)
- The double standard, side by side: a broken bone gets a casserole and a signed cast;
  emotional pain gets "have you tried going outside." The line: *"We hand someone drowning
  a map of the shoreline instead of a rope."*
- The reframe — the reaction line: no one blinks at hiring a **personal trainer** to get
  physically fit, but hiring a **therapist** to get mentally fit carries stigma. Same logic,
  opposite social response. This is the argumentative peak.
- The proof: a restrained browser mockup of the live An Injustice! article — headline
  "The Silent Killer No One Talks About" with the publication name visible — as social proof
  that the argument landed somewhere real.

## Outro / punchline
The closing line of the essay, held clean over a quiet close-conversation photo:
**"Vulnerability isn't the opposite of strength. It's proof of it."**

## User flow worth showing
none — landing-page only. No app or flow exists. Centerpiece is the writing plus the
published-headline proof; the video relies on the Key moments above.

## Tone
- Preset: polished
- Creative direction: quiet, editorial, human — a piece of writing that got recognized
- Interpretation: fewer scenes, longer holds, restraint over energy. Pacing is calm; type
  is large and still; no chaos, no parody. The subject is mental health, so the video earns
  attention by conviction, not by motion.

## Audio direction
- Role: warm, sparse intimate bed
- Music: soft ambient piano / low warm pad, unobtrusive. NOT the skill's bundled
  happy-beats-business-moves tracks (upbeat corporate — wrong for this subject). Source a
  warm ambient bed; if none available, low music is acceptable but silence is a valid
  alternative for the closing line.
- Music treatment: enter low under the hook, hold flat through the middle, a gentle upward
  settle at the publication reveal (Scene 3), fade under the final line (Scene 4).
- Music cue guidance: no bundled preset fits; cues to be detected at composition time. Target
  one soft swell at ~14s (the An Injustice! reveal). No beat-grid sequential text in this
  video — reveals are slow single holds, so no beat snapping required. Restraint note: this
  is a mental-health piece; keep everything under the words, never over them.
- Audio-reactive treatment: none (or barely-perceptible glow breathing on the amber accent);
  no waveform bars, no energy-driven motion — restraint is the point.
- SFX posture: near-silent. At most one soft page/settle at the article reveal.
- Audio-coupled moments: the publication reveal (soft settle) is the only audio-coupled beat.
- Restraint rule: audio must never become "content" — no drops, no build, no percussive
  business energy, nothing that turns a serious argument into a promo.

## Storyboard

### Scene 1 — The inherited lie (hook) — 5s
Full-bleed warm window-light photo (person by a window), amber cast + grain + vignette.
Large Playfair Display line lands and holds: **"Real men don't cry."** A small muted Inter
sub-label fades under it near the end: *the lie we inherited.* The line must stay fully
settled ~2s to sting.
Sequential/interaction: none.
Audio intent: enter low and warm; let the silence around the line carry the weight.
Audio-coupled idea: none.
Music: soft ambient bed, very low.
Transition mood: soft crossfade → Scene 2

### Scene 2 — The double standard — 6s
Photo of two men on a bench. Two short editorial-emphasis lines, held one after the other
(not beat-snapped — each holds to its read floor): a broken bone gets a signed cast and a
casserole; emotional pain gets *"have you tried going outside."* Then the amber pull-quote
lands: **"We hand someone drowning a map of the shoreline instead of a rope."**
Sequential/interaction: yes — two contrast lines appear in sequence, each held ~1.5s fully
visible before the next; then the pull-quote holds ~1.8s. Do not rush.
Audio intent: hold flat and quiet; no swell — this is the ache.
Audio-coupled idea: none (soft settle on the pull-quote only if it feels natural).
Music: unchanged low bed.
Transition mood: soft crossfade → Scene 3

### Scene 3 — The reframe + the proof — 5s
Two halves of one idea. First: the reaction line as large type — **personal trainer** for
the body, no stigma / **therapist** for the mind, stigma — *same logic, opposite response.*
Then it resolves into a restrained browser mockup of the live An Injustice! article: headline
**"The Silent Killer No One Talks About"** with the publication name **An Injustice!** and the
URL visible, presented factually as social proof (no "featured!" inflation, no fake metrics).
Sequential/interaction: yes — the trainer/therapist contrast resolves into the article mockup
with a single soft settle as the headline lands. Hold the headline ~1.8s so it's readable.
Audio intent: the one permitted lift — a gentle upward settle as the published headline
appears. Recognition, not triumph.
Audio-coupled idea: one soft page/settle SFX as the article mockup resolves.
Music: subtle warm swell up, then back down.
Transition mood: soft crossfade → Scene 4

### Scene 4 — The closing line (outro) — 4s
Quiet close-conversation photo, dimmed. The essay's final line lands in Playfair Display,
the key word in sage `#86efac` (the niche's resolution accent):
**"Vulnerability isn't the opposite of strength. It's proof of it."** Hold clean ~2s. Optional
final muted credit line: *An Injustice! · 2026.* Fade to warm brown.
Sequential/interaction: none — one line, one long hold.
Audio intent: fade the bed under the line; let it end on near-silence.
Audio-coupled idea: none.
Music: fade out.
Transition mood: slow crossfade to end.
