# Brag Plan: "The ₹20-Lakh Hire" (cinematic-data-driven) — for the decisions-under-pressure essay

**Chosen hook:** *"Stop trusting your gut — it cost me a ₹20-lakh hire it loved."*
**Hook formula:** contrarian + hyper-specificity — leads with the essay's own thesis reversal ("trust your gut" is wrong), then grounds it in one concrete lived number so it reads as a real moment, not a guru line.
**Tone preset:** freeform — *cinematic-data-driven* (mapped to the built-in `cinematic` preset for pacing/transitions).
**Angle (one line):** A gut-vs-the-sheet showdown told cinematically — the charismatic hire the gut screamed yes to, the scored rubric that graded him a 61, the ₹20-lakh mistake it caught, landing on the one question that replaces instinct: *what would I have to see to know I'm wrong.*

## What is this?
Not an app — a personal essay published on Medium: **"Stop Trusting Your Gut: The Case for Making Decisions Under Pressure Without It."** The brag is real writing, real stakes, real numbers: a 10-year data scientist arguing that under pressure the gut reads the *size of the number, not the shape of the work* — and proving it with a scored hiring rubric that caught a ₹20-lakh mistake his instinct was walking him straight into.

## The angle
The essay's spine is a duel between two ways of deciding: the loud, certain gut and the quiet, calm sheet. The video stages that duel. It opens by inverting the most-repeated advice in the room ("trust your gut"), drops the reader into the interview where the gut said an unqualified *yes*, reveals the rubric score that overruled it (**61**, cutoff **75**), names the cost the sheet caught (**₹20 lakh**), and resolves on the reframe that replaces gut-feel entirely: you don't ask what this *feels* like — you ask what you'd have to see to know you're wrong, and you go look. Cinematic-data-driven means the drama lives in slow push-ins and warm light, but the *proof* is numeric — the score and the rupee figure are shown as data, not decoration.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
Warm full-bleed canvas, slow push-in (scale 1.0→1.06). The line lands fast and holds: **"Stop trusting your gut —"** then completes: **"it cost me a ₹20-lakh hire it loved."** Amber emphasis on **₹20-lakh**. This is the contrarian reversal + the specific number in one breath. Longest-held line of the opening so it reads completely.

## Key moments (the middle)
- **The interview the gut loved** — restrained line over a warm canvas: *"Sharp in the room. Great energy. Every instinct said hire him."* Sets up the gut's verdict so the reveal can overrule it. Amber on **hire him**.
- **The sheet overrules the gut** — the data beat, staged as a scorecard: candidate scores **61** against a hard cutoff of **75**. The two numbers arrive one after the other; **61** lands under a **75** line, then a struck-through / "PASSED" mark. This is the cinematic-data-driven centerpiece — the rubric as evidence.
- **The reframe** — the essay's sharpest line as a type-only pull-quote: *"My gut was measuring charisma. The sheet was measuring whether they could do the job."* The argumentative peak.

## Outro / punchline
Warm canvas, dimmed. The one-question reframe in Playfair Display, resolution phrase in sage `#86efac`:
**"Don't ask what it feels like. Ask what you'd have to see to know you're wrong."**
The beat before fade: the real published headline + Medium as social proof that the piece landed somewhere real.
