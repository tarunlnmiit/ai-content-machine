# Brag Plan: "One Number That Kept Repeating" (cinematic) — for the 1,003-job-postings essay

**Chosen hook:** "1,003 postings, 30 days, one number that kept repeating."
**Formula:** Hyper-specificity + Numbered list (three concrete numbers as a trailer title card; "one number that kept repeating" opens a curiosity loop the video pays off at the 87% reveal). This is also the essay's own opening image caption — the author's already-chosen frame.
**Tone preset:** cinematic
**Angle (one line):** A data-driven cinematic reveal of the gap between what's *hyped* (LangChain, in 3% of postings) and what's *hired* (Python + ML, in the vast majority) — 1,003 real postings scraped over 30 days, told like a trailer where the numbers are the drama.

---

## What is this?
Not an app — a data-journalism essay by a 10-year data scientist: **"I Scraped 1,003 Data Science Job Postings — 87% Want This One Thing, and Most Candidates Still Don't Say It."** The brag is: real scrape, real counts, real methodology (flaws included) — a 30-day dataset that quietly dismantles the LangChain/deep-learning hype and shows what recruiters actually write into the requisition.

## The angle
The essay's own turn, told cinematically: three specific numbers open like a title card, the 87% reveal lands the "boring" winner (strong Python + ML fundamentals), then the sharpest reframe in the piece — LangChain, the framework that dominated AI-engineering discourse for two years, appeared in 3% of postings (36 of 1,003). The drama lives in the count-ups and the hyped-vs-hired contrast, not in fast cutting. It closes on the essay's thesis line and, honestly, on the real article headline as proof — no fabricated publication, no invented URL.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
Dark navy data canvas with a faint scanning-grid motion, slow push-in. The title-card line lands fast and holds: **"1,003 postings. 30 days. One number that kept repeating."** — the three numbers (`1,003`, `30`, and the withheld "one number") in accent blue/cyan against off-white. The withheld number is the promise the 87% scene pays off.

## Key moments (the middle)
- **The 87% reveal.** A dramatic count-up `0 → 87%`, then the anticlimax that IS the point: *"Not deep learning. Not the framework trending on X. Strong Python + machine-learning fundamentals."* The winner is deliberately boring — cyan/green for the stat.
- **Hyped vs hired.** The essay's sharpest line, staged as a two-number slam: *"LangChain. Hyped for two straight years."* → **"3%."** (36 of 1,003) in code-red. The gap between what's loud and what's in the requisition.
- **The payoff.** *"The fix wasn't learning a new skill. It was saying the one they already had — by name and number."* Land the concrete result from the essay: recruiter replies jumped, two interviews in the same week.

## Outro / punchline
Dark canvas, dimmed grid. The essay's thesis line in large Inter: **"The gap between what's loud on Twitter and what's actually in the requisition is where most job searches quietly go wrong."** Then a restrained proof card with the real article headline — no publication logo invented, no URL bar fabricated.

## User flow worth showing
none — no app or flow exists. The centerpiece is the data (count-ups, the 87%/3% contrast) plus the published-headline proof.

## Tone
- Preset: cinematic
- Creative direction: a data story told like a trailer — big type, dramatic count-ups, restrained motion, numbers as the drama
- Interpretation: five scenes, full-bleed dark canvas, large type, slow push-ins and count-ups rather than quick cuts. The urgency is in the reveal of each number, not in cut speed. Every stat holds long enough to read.

## Format: vertical — 1080x1920
## Duration: ~21s
Five cinematic scenes at 4–4.5s each. Count-ups and the 87%/3% stats each need settled read time, which drives duration to the upper-middle of the 15–25s band.

## Share copy (draft)
I scraped 1,003 data science job postings over 30 days. 87% wanted the same boring skill — and LangChain, hyped for two years straight, showed up in exactly 3%. The gap between what's loud online and what's actually hired is bigger than you'd think.

## Audio direction
- Role: cinematic support — a low, modern ambient-tech bed with a single swell at the 87% reveal and a percussive hit on the "3%" slam
- Music: cinematic/tech mood (not warm acoustic — this is the DS niche). Enters low under the hook, holds tense through the count-up, swells as `87%` completes, hits hard on `3%`, then settles under the thesis line so the words land last.
- Music treatment: enter at low volume under the hook (~0.20–0.24), lift at the 87% count-up completion, accent the 3% reveal, fade under the closing line (1.5s fade-in, ~2.0s fade-out, trimmed to exact duration).
- Music cue guidance: track TBD (bundled cinematic/tech bed). Strong cues to target: (1) the `0→87%` count-up completion (~Scene 2 end), (2) the `3%` slam (~Scene 3 mid). Sequential number reveals in Scene 3 should land on strong beats but each stat holds to its read floor — snap the label, hold the number. Detect exact cue timestamps at composition time if no preset exists.
- Audio-reactive treatment: subtle — the canvas grid/glow may breathe gently with music RMS at the swell; no waveform bars, no gimmicks.
- SFX posture: sparse and motion-matched — one tick/settle as the count-up locks, one low impact on the `3%` slam. Professional restraint.
- Audio-coupled moments: the `0→87%` count-up (ticks that resolve on lock), the `3%` slam (impact), the headline proof card (soft settle).
- Restraint rule: no dense SFX layering, no meme stingers. This is a trailer for a data story, not a hype reel — the numbers carry it.
