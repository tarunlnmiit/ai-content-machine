# Brag Plan: "7 Simple Habits" — as an app-store listing (app-store tone)

**Chosen hook:** "7 habits. No app. No subscription. Still beat your $200 stack."
**Formula:** numbered list + contrarian (hooks pick #9 — seeds the app-store parody in the first frame: the count promises a list, the "no app / no subscription" line is the contrarian jab at productivity software).
**Tone preset:** app-store.
**One-line angle:** Present the essay's 7 no-app habits as an ironic app-store listing — five stars, free, no subscription, no app required — where the "product" is a scrap of paper and the killer spec is that it survives bad days.

## What is this?
Not an app — a personal essay published on Medium: **"7 Simple Habits That Quietly Rewired My Productivity"** (Life & Self-Development, 2026-06-08). The piece argues that the most productive people don't run complicated systems; they run a handful of habits simple enough to do on bad days — seven of them, ranked by impact (shutdown ritual, weekly brain dump, one frog, the done list, one 90-minute block, tomorrow's top three written tonight, one metric per week). None require an app. That's the whole point, and it's the whole joke of the brag: we're "launching" the anti-app as if it were an app.

## The angle
The essay mocks Notion templates, color-coded calendars, and $200 productivity stacks. So the video adopts the exact visual language it's mocking — a clean, confident app-store product listing — and turns it against itself. The "app" is seven habits on paper. The rating is five stars. The price is free, no in-app purchases, no subscription. The centerpiece "feature cards" are three of the sharpest habits. The punchline is the one spec no dashboard can claim: *it works on bad days.* The listing card doubles as social proof — the product name IS the real published headline.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
"**7 habits.** No app. No subscription. **Still beat your $200 stack.**" — Playfair Display, "No app. No subscription." emphasized in amber. Fast slam-in, then hold to the reading floor. This sets the ironic app-store frame instantly: it sounds like a product boast, but the product has no app.

## Key moments (the middle)
- **The listing header** — an app-store-style product card: product name = the real essay headline "7 Simple Habits That Quietly Rewired My Productivity", ★★★★★, and the mock metadata "Free · No subscription · No app required." The parody lands here.
- **Three feature cards, arriving one by one** — the highest-impact habits framed as app features:
  - *The Shutdown Ritual* — close the day on purpose. 4 minutes.
  - *Eat One Frog* — do the task you're avoiding, first.
  - *The 90-Minute Block* — one, protected, uncancellable.
- **The spec strip** — the remaining four as a compact "what's included" list (Weekly Brain Dump · The Done List · Tomorrow's Top Three · One Metric a Week), held long enough to scan, reinforcing "seven, and every one is free."

## Outro / punchline
The contrarian turn as the killer spec, then the resolution line in sage. The listing's fake five-star review becomes the real thesis: **"★★★★★ — Works on bad days. That's the only spec that matters."** → final line: **"No template survives an imperfect life. These seven do."**

## User flow worth showing
none — essay, no app or flow exists. The "product listing" is a deliberate parody frame, not a recreation of a real UI. Centerpiece is the feature-card treatment of the habits plus the published-headline proof.

## Tone
- Preset: app-store
- Creative direction: an ironic, straight-faced product launch for the anti-product — clean feature cards for a thing that is deliberately not software
- Interpretation: clean, confident, feature-forward pacing; title-case medium-weight type; smooth slide/wipe transitions (0.35-0.45s), nothing dramatic. The humor comes from playing the listing completely straight — the more polished the "app store" chrome, the sharper the punchline that there's no app.

## Format: vertical — 1080x1920
## Duration: ~20s (five scenes)

- Background: `#1a1208` (warm deep brown) / card bg (glass) `rgba(35,26,14,0.85)`, border `rgba(245,158,11,0.18)`

## Audio direction
- Role: clean, confident, warm acoustic bed — kept low; nothing swells. Composed and bright, matching the app-store polish.
- Music: "I Just Want Quiet" (slow acoustic, No Copyright) — `v1/assets/audio/bgm/life/itYCLFXGQMc_[No Copyright Music] I Just Want Quiet - Slow Acou.mp3`, fresh offset (~80s in) to differ from sibling Life builds.
- Music treatment: trim to ~20s, `volume≈0.20, afade in ~1.2s, afade out ~1.8s` under the final line so the words land last. Root-level `<audio class="clip">`.
- Music cue guidance: bundled acoustic track, gentle ~70-90 BPM feel. Target one soft strong-cue landing near the hook resolution (~2.8s) and one near the outro line (~16.5s). For the three feature cards (Scene 3), use a relaxed beat-grid window ~5.5-9.5s but snap card reveals to every OTHER beat so each card title clears its ~0.8s reading floor — readability wins over rhythm.
- Audio-reactive treatment: none / subtle — at most a faint amber presence-breathe on the listing card; no waveform bars, no reactive chrome.
- SFX posture: sparse, professional restraint. A soft, clean "settle" tick as each of the three feature cards arrives; one gentle chime as the five-star rating lands. Nothing arcade-y — this is a premium listing, not a game.
- Audio-coupled moments: the three feature cards arriving one by one (Scene 3); the five-star rating populating on the listing header (Scene 2); the final sage line landing into near-silence.
- Restraint rule: no swell, no drop, no percussive hype. The audio must stay quiet and composed — the joke is in how seriously the listing is presented, and loud music would break the deadpan.

Audio-coupled idea: gentle chime as the fifth star lands.
Music: bed continues, low.
Transition mood: clean slide → Scene 3

Audio intent: each arrival feels deliberate and premium, like features being unveiled.
Audio-coupled idea: card-by-card settle SFX, snapped to every other beat (~0.9s apart) for readability, not rushed.

### Scene 4 — What's included (spec strip) — 4.0s
Compact "What's included" spec list — the remaining four habits as clean Inter rows on the glass panel: "Weekly Brain Dump · The Done List · Tomorrow's Top Three · One Metric a Week". Eyebrow label in muted `#a0856a`: "ALSO INCLUDED — still free." Held long enough to scan all four.
Sequential/interaction: rows fade in as a group (or quick top-to-bottom), then hold the full set for the scan floor — this is scannable copy, not one-by-one drama.
Audio intent: quiet continuation; "and there's more, all free" without hype.
Audio-coupled idea: none / one soft group-settle tick.
Music: bed continues, low.
Transition mood: clean slide → Scene 5

### Scene 5 — The killer spec (outro) — 4.0s
The turn: a mock five-star "review" that carries the real thesis, then the resolution. First: "**★★★★★ — Works on bad days. That's the only spec that matters.**" (amber stars, off-white line). Then the final line in sage `#86efac`: "**No template survives an imperfect life. These seven do.**" Hold clean, then fade to warm brown.
Sequential/interaction: review line lands first, then the sage resolution line beneath it.
Audio intent: settle to near-silence so the sage line lands last and quietest.
Audio-coupled idea: the sage line landing into the fade — no SFX, just the word.
Music: fade out (~1.8s) under the final line.
Transition mood: slow crossfade to end.
