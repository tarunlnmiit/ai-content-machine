# Brag Plan: "The Cost of Carrying Things Nobody Sees"

**Chosen hook:** "There's a version of you most people will never meet."
**Hook formula:** POV + curiosity gap (implied second-person POV — *you* have this hidden version — wrapped in a curiosity gap; it's also the essay's actual opening line, which keeps the video grounded in the real text and reads cleanly sound-off).
**Tone preset:** polished
**Angle (one line):** Open on the version of you nobody meets, name the invisible cognitive tax it silently charges your work and your relationships, and land on the first move — stop calling the weight nothing.

## What is this app?
Not an app — a personal essay on the hidden exhaustion that comes not from doing too
much but from *holding* too much: the unresolved things you carry in silence, the
cognitive tax they charge in the background, the way that weight leaks into the people
around you, and why the first step to putting it down is simply refusing to call it nothing.

## The angle
A quiet argument delivered as a piece of writing, not a product demo. The video walks the
essay's own turn — from the version of you nobody meets, to the fog and lost forty minutes
that carrying it produces, to the line a person close to the author said ("You're here, but
you're not really here anymore"), to the reframe that names why we keep carrying, and finally
to the essay's first move: stop calling the weight nothing. Specific to this piece, not
generic: it uses the essay's actual opening line, its actual lived images (rereading the same
paragraph, the "you're not really here" conversation), and its social proof is the real
published headline, not a stat.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
The essay's opening line, stated flat and restrained: **"There's a version of you most
people will never meet."** in Playfair Display over the warm window-light photo (person
sitting alone, contemplative). No motion tricks — the line holds long enough to register.

## Key moments (the middle)
- The cognitive tax made concrete, over the dimmed photo of a person at a desk:
  *"You reread the same paragraph three times and called it a bad day. You weren't lazy —
  you were running something heavy in the background."*
- The leak into relationships — the essay's most quietly devastating line as a Playfair
  italic pull-quote: *"You're here, but you're not really here anymore."* This is the
  emotional peak.
- The reframe that names why we keep carrying: *"You don't keep carrying it because you're
  weak. You keep carrying it because putting it down means looking at it."*

## Outro / punchline
The essay's first-move line, held clean, the resolution phrase in sage `#86efac`:
**"The first move isn't to fix it. It's to stop calling it nothing."**

## User flow worth showing
none — no app or flow exists. The centerpiece is the writing plus the published-headline
proof; the video relies on the Key moments above.

## Tone
- Preset: polished
- Creative direction: quiet, editorial, human — a private exhaustion named with care
- Interpretation: fewer scenes, longer holds, restraint over energy. Pacing is calm; type is
  large and still; no chaos, no parody. The subject is invisible emotional weight, so the
  video earns attention by conviction and recognition, not by motion.

built for vertical.

## Audio direction
- Role: warm, sparse intimate bed
- Music: soft ambient acoustic ("I Just Want Quiet"), volume ~0.20, unobtrusive. Enter low
  under the hook, hold flat through the middle, fade under the closing line.
- Music treatment: 1.5s fade in, 2.0s fade out; no swell, no drop — restraint is the point.
- Music cue guidance: track "I Just Want Quiet" (slow acoustic). Target one soft settle around
  the reframe→outro handoff (~18-19s); exact timestamp to be detected at composition time. No
  beat-grid sequential reveals in this storyboard, so no beat window needed. Restraint note:
  the subject is private grief — keep cues near-inaudible; audio must never announce itself.
- Audio-reactive treatment: none. No waveform bars, no energy-driven motion.
- SFX posture: none. The subject does not want a "settle" sound effect.
- Restraint rule: audio must never become "content" — nothing that turns a serious argument
  into a promo.
