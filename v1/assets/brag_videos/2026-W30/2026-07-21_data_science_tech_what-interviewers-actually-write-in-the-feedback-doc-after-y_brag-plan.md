# Brag Plan: "What Interviewers Actually Write in the Feedback Doc"

> **Chosen hook:** *"'Couldn't tell me why they picked that model.' That sentence sank him."*
> **Formula:** Curiosity gap + Cold open — opens on the literal verdict (result-first) while withholding *why* one sentence could sink a strong candidate.
> **Tone preset:** cinematic
> **Angle:** A confessional pull from the scoring chair — an interviewer reads back the real, verbatim lines from 40 hiring loops to reveal that the feedback doc grades judgment, not correctness. The one line that sinks you, the one that saves you, and the line they'll write down about you next.

## What is this app?
Not an app — a first-person essay by a data scientist who's run ~40 interview loops, revealing what actually gets written in the feedback doc after a candidate leaves the room. The thesis: interviewers aren't grading whether you know the answer; they're recording whether they'd trust your judgment when there's no answer key. The brag is real writing that names the exact phrases that decide a hire.

## The angle
The video treats the essay as a confession from the interviewer's chair. It uses the essay's own verbatim feedback-doc lines — the sentences a candidate never gets to read — as the centerpiece. Not generic career advice: every on-screen line is pulled directly from the piece. Cold-opens on the sentence that sank a strong candidate, turns to the sentence that saved a weaker one, and lands on the single line the reader can act on before their next loop.

## Hook (first 2-3 seconds)
Full-bleed on deep navy, in ExtraBold display type, the verdict lands first: **"'Couldn't tell me why they picked that model.'"** — then, a beat later, the reframe below it: **That one sentence sank the hire.** The room's judgment stated as fact before any context. It holds long enough to read completely; the withheld *why* pulls the next 15 seconds.

## Key moments (the middle)
- **The reveal that reframes everything:** *"Interviewers aren't grading whether you know the answer. They're scoring whether they'd trust your judgment when there's no answer key."* The essay's core thesis as a dramatic full-screen declarative.
- **The line that saves you:** *"Volunteered the failure mode before I asked."* — the candidate who caught her own leaked feature. Paired with the verdict: *got the offer over a stronger resume.*
- **The no-hire:** *"Answers are memorized, not owned."* — the guy who nailed every question cold and still got flagged. Proof that a clean transcript isn't the score.

## Outro / punchline
The one actionable line, held clean: **"Stop rehearsing answers. Find the story of the time you were wrong — and learn to tell it without flinching."** Then the resolution beat in code-green: *"That's the line they'll write down."* Followed by a restrained honest-proof card — the real published headline as social proof that this is a piece a real audience read.

## User flow worth showing
none — no app or flow exists. Centerpiece is the essay's verbatim feedback-doc lines plus the published-headline proof.

## Tone
- Preset: cinematic
- Creative direction: confessional trailer from the scoring chair — the feedback doc read aloud

Restraint over energy; the tension is that these are the sentences you were never supposed to see. Scenes enter at 0.95 scale and reach 1.0.

## Audio direction
- Role: cinematic support — a low, tense bed that carries the confessional weight without melodrama
- Music: a restrained cinematic underscore — slow, minor, a quiet pulse (not upbeat, not chaotic). Enter low under the hook, hold flat through the middle reveals, allow one small swell into the outro, then settle under the closing line so the words land last.
- Music treatment: start under the hook at low volume; hold steady through Scenes 2–4; a subtle rise entering Scene 5; ease back so "That's the line they'll write down" lands in near-quiet.
- Music cue guidance: track to be selected at composition time. Target one strong cue at the hook's verdict-reframe reveal (~2s), and one at the Scene 5 swell (proof/outro). No beat-grid sequential reveals needed — lines land one at a time, held to read floor, not snapped to a beat.
- Audio-reactive treatment: subtle — use the bed's low energy to let the data-grid backdrop breathe faintly; no waveform bars, no aggressive reactivity.
- SFX posture: sparse, professional restraint. At most: one soft, dry "stamp" as each verdict (green HIRE / red NO-HIRE) lands, and a faint key/caret tick if a line types in. Nothing playful.
- Audio-coupled moments: the verdict stamps (green/red) and the optional caret tick on the hook line.
- Restraint rule: no percussive drops, no riser-into-whoosh cliché, no music louder than the words. Silence under the final line is preferred over a swell that steps on it.
