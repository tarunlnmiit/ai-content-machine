# Poetry Carousel — Direction Routing

Four locked template packs, all built from the approved "genuinely distinct design directions"
round (see the source mockups' `INDEX.md`). All four stay in rotation — pick ONE per carousel based
on the poem's own content, never by round-robin or by default preference.

| Direction | File | World |
|---|---|---|
| D1 — Draft, Unsent | `d1.md` | The poem as an actual unsent iOS Notes draft — struck-through edits, a highlighted phrase, a blinking cursor. |
| D2 — The Poem Is The Page | `d2.md` | Typography-first editorial — massive scale-contrast Playfair Display carries the whole composition, near-zero decoration. |
| D3 — After Hours | `d3.md` | Dark luxury verse — ink-black field, single gold hairline/dot/eyebrow used once per slide, museum wall-label restraint. |
| D4 — Room to Breathe | `d4.md` | Japanese-minimal *ma* — asymmetric composition, vast negative space, verse pushed to the margin with a bronze seal mark. |

## Content → Direction routing rubric

- **D1 — Draft, Unsent**: confessional poems, poems about unsaid words, poems that read like
  something the poet almost didn't send — private, unfinished-feeling, first-person address to
  someone specific. If the poem's central image is a phone, a note, a text never sent, or an edit
  the poet made to their own feelings, route here.
- **D2 — The Poem Is The Page**: declarative, aphoristic verse — poems that already sound like a
  thesis statement, poems built on one turn or one punch-line word, poems short enough that a
  single line can dominate a frame without padding. If the poem would work as a pull-quote on its
  own, route here.
- **D3 — After Hours**: grief, night, loss, endings — poems that want to be read once, alone, in a
  dark room, and returned to later. If the poem's register is heavy, elegiac, or ceremonial, route
  here; the rationed gold reads as reverence, not decoration.
- **D4 — Room to Breathe**: stillness, space, poems about presence/absence or what's left unsaid by
  omission rather than by editing — poems whose subject IS emptiness, distance, or quiet. If the
  poem is about a room, a pause, a person who is there but not really there, route here.

## Default pick and reason

**Default: D1 — Draft, Unsent.** It is the most format-agnostic of the four — it works for any
poem that has a clear narrative "I" and a moment of private reflection, which covers the majority
of the niche's output, and it earns saves/screenshots through recognizability rather than requiring
a poem with an unusually strong single line (D2), an unusually heavy register (D3), or an
unusually spatial subject (D4). When a poem doesn't obviously match one of the other three rubric
entries, default to D1 rather than forcing a weaker fit elsewhere.
