# DS Carousel Direction Packs — Routing Rubric

Four locked template packs, all approved and kept in rotation (`d1.md`–`d4.md`). One is chosen per
carousel based on what the underlying blog/post actually is — never rotate mechanically or pick by
personal preference; route by content shape.

## D1 — Backchannel (`d1.md`)

Route here when the post is **insider/gossip framing** — "what [the other side] actually say
about you," a behind-the-scenes conversation the reader wasn't meant to see, panel/peer
deliberation, or any angle whose hook is fundamentally "here's the private conversation." The
two-panelist DM format only works when the content genuinely has (or can be staged as having) two
distinct voices reacting to something — don't force it onto a single-author essay.

## D2 — The Verdict (`d2.md`)

Route here when the post is a **single strong thesis or opinion essay** — one argument, cleanly
stated, that doesn't need proof-images, code, or a leaked-document framing to land. Best for posts
whose core move is a reframe ("it's not X, it's Y") or a sharp definitional distinction. If the
post's power is in its sentence-level writing rather than its evidence, this is the direction.

## D3 — Root Access (`d3.md`)

Route here when the post is **about code, debugging, or a technical process** — anything with a
genuine before/after, a diff, a prompt, a metric table, or content that a working data
scientist/engineer would recognize from their own terminal. Mandatory when the post's central beat
is literally a comparison (memorized vs. owned, wrong query vs. right query) — D3's Git Diff
archetype was built for exactly that shape and nothing else renders it as convincingly.

## D4 — Redacted Memo (`d4.md`)

Route here when the post is an **exposé, "rules they don't tell you," or leaked-truth framing** —
content whose hook is fundamentally "here is the internal document you weren't cleared to read."
Works well for hiring-process/HR-adjacent content, industry-insider rules, or any post structured
as a set of numbered facts/exhibits rather than a narrative.

## Default

**D1 — Backchannel.** It's the direction that already extends the one slide the client explicitly
liked in the original review round, it degrades gracefully for almost any two-sided content (setup
line + reaction), and its native-UI realism reads as credible even when the underlying post has no
strong single angle to lean on. Use it whenever a post doesn't clearly match D2/D3/D4's more
specific triggers above.
