---

### The 2019 SEO paradox

The 15 most-viewed articles are ALL from 2019–2020. Average read ratio: **~27%**. These got views through Medium's recommendation engine and Google SEO — not curation. None would pass the >50% threshold today. The lesson: high view counts from that era were distribution-driven, not engagement-driven. **Don't optimise for that model — it no longer exists.**

---

### What actually drives high read ratios

Articles at 40%+ share these title properties:

- **Specific function / action in the title**: "Structuring a NodeJS API in an efficient way" (44%), "How You Can Have Sustainably High Levels of Dopamine" (44%), "How Much Medium Paid Me for 100,000 Views" (42%)
- **Provocation or challenge**: "If You Are a Serious Poet, Stop Writing Poetry Online" (31%, but highest earnings-per-view by far at $140)
- **Emotional specificity**: "You Are Setting Yourself for Hurt by Expecting Help from Others" (41%), "I Wish I Had Known This Way to Process My Thoughts" (55%)
- **Concrete curiosity gap**: "JavaScript's Magical Tips Every Developer Should Remember" (41%)

Articles at <25% share these title properties:

- **Generic question format**: "What is Love? What is Hate?" (15%), "What Is Neural Network Implementation, Anyway?" (20%)
- **Abstract noun + vague claim**: "Therapy Taught Me About Expectation Management" (18%), "How Being Stuck in a Situation Can Do Wonders" (19%)
- **Fluffy self-help framing**: "Here's My Secret Sauce on How to Be Consistent" (21%), "30-Day 30-Pubs Submission Challenge" (21%)

### The "Serious Poet" outlier — title with provocation beats title with description

"If You Are a Serious Poet, Stop Writing Poetry Online" (Jan 2022): 3.5K views, 31% read ratio, **$140.81 earned**.

$140 on 3.5K views = **$40 per 1,000 views**. The next closest is "Node.js Coding Style Guidelines" at $263 on 65K views = **$4 per 1,000 views**. The provocation-framed title got this into a high-paying publication tier. The actual read ratio (31%) is average — but the title brought the right reader, who stayed long enough to count as earnings.

Implication: a good provocative title matters as much for publication placement as for clicks.

### Niche patterns

**DS/tech:**
- Tutorial-style reference pieces get high read ratios when titles are hyper-specific ("Zero Frequency Problem" 52%, "Types & Characteristics of Data Sets" 47%)
- Generic "Understanding X" or "Intro to X" titles underperform (20–28%)
- The single best formula: action/technique + specific outcome

**Life:**
- Enormous spread: 15% to 55%
- Articles with emotional specificity (naming a sensation, a moment) beat abstract self-help
- "I Wish I Had Known This Way to Process My Thoughts" (55%) vs "Therapy Taught Me About Expectation Management" (18%) — both therapy-adjacent topics, opposite outcomes

**Poetry:**
- Short pieces with evocative titles cluster around 29–42%
- Index pages and titled collections ("Index: Poetry — Tarun Gupta") get near-zero reads (3%) — never publish these as standalone stories

Articles published in 2026 have very low absolute view counts (2–375 views). Read ratios will stabilise only after 30+ days of distribution. The view ceiling for recent articles is artificially low — do not conclude these are failures. ## Actionable rules derived from this data

1. **Avoid titles with two nouns and no tension.** "Understanding X" → rewrite as "[What X] Cost Me" or "The X That [Happened]"
2. **The Dopamine benchmark:** 44% is the target read ratio for life content. If a draft's first paragraph test fails, the article will not reach 44%.
3. **Short technical pieces beat long ones on read ratio** — 1–2 min reads often hit 45–54% because readers self-select accurately
4. **Provocative life titles earn more per view** — the Serious Poet piece is the clearest proof
5. **Poetry titles: name the sensation, not the subject.** "Loneliness" (32%) vs "The Need For Closure" (44%) — the latter names the exact psychological state
