# Poetry Niche Virality Formula
## Reference account: @christi.steyn (~100k+ followers, spoken-word poetry)

Reverse-engineered from deep reel-by-reel analysis (6 reels opened, all captions read, comment sections studied). Covers her full range of formats — not just the highest performer — because different formats serve different goals. Use alongside `00_playbook.md` and `01_hook_library.md`.

---

## The core insight: the poem is a projective surface

Christi's poems don't tell the reader how to feel. They describe a feeling so precisely that the reader projects their own experience onto the poem and feels *seen*. The viral mechanism is not "this is a good poem" — it is **"someone finally said the thing I couldn't say."**

The result: the comment section becomes people sharing their own love stories, loss, or memories. The poem starts the conversation; the community finishes it.

## The 5 formats (pick by content type)

### FORMAT 1 — Full poem in caption, face to camera (HIGHEST PERFORMER)
**Best example:** 370k "gifts"

**Visual:** Close-up face, warm golden-hour or soft indoor light. Eyes slightly off-camera (reading/performing). Intimate, as if speaking only to you.

**Audio:** Original audio (her voice). The performance IS the content.

**Caption structure:**
```
[Full poem — stanzas separated by blank lines, no preamble]

[Author name]
```

No hashtags. No CTA. No "link in bio." The poem and nothing else.

**The poem structure that hit 370k:**
- Form: 4 stanzas × 4 lines each
- Pattern: 3 stanzas of "I would [impossible grand gesture] / but [concrete reality blocks it]"
- Stanza 4: turn — "therefore I give you my heart" — the offer that IS possible
- Loose ABAB rhyme scheme
- Each stanza pairs a romantic fantasy with a mundane/playful detail that grounds it:
  - "but bird language is not on duolingo" — humor inside tenderness
  - "but my hands are only flesh" — vulnerability inside ambition
- No abstraction — every line has a physical object: moon, pillow, birds, star, flesh, key

**Why this format produces saves:** the full poem in the caption is what people screenshot and send to a partner. "Read this." The video is the entry point; the caption is what they keep.

---

### FORMAT 2 — Hook line in caption, poem entirely in audio
**Best examples:** 88.8k "BE DANGEROUS LADIES," 77.6k "can we suffer together, my love?"

**The idea:** don't give away the poem in the caption. Make them listen. The caption's job is to create enough intrigue to press play and stay for the audio.

**Caption variants:**

*Variant A — Enticing hook line only:*
```
can we suffer together, my love?

#poetry #spokenpoetry #vows
```
One unanswered question. 3 hashtags max. Nothing more.

*Variant B — Conversational, imperfect, authentic:*
```
Omg I have to record this again later I had no time! I pronounced "squawk" so American it's embarrassing.

Love you
BE DANGEROUS LADIES
LOVE YOU
```
This is meta-commentary on the process + the rallying phrase from the poem in all-caps at the end. The all-caps line IS the poem's emotional core — it makes people screenshot the caption even without the poem text.

**Why this format works:** the caption creates intimacy and curiosity at the same time. "BE DANGEROUS LADIES" with no context is arresting. "can we suffer together, my love?" sounds like a radical love vow. The question activates before they've heard a word.

---

### FORMAT 3 — Single line + emoji, intimate visual
**Best example:** 63.9k "your home, is my arms 💜"

**Visual:** B&W photograph — couple embracing, cinematic. Or a couple photograph with strong contrast and emotion.

**Caption:**
```
your home, is my arms 💜
```
One poetic line. One emoji. Nothing else.

**Why this format produces comments:** the line is minimal enough to be a projective surface. People drop into the comments and share their own version — a memory, a current love, a person they lost. 5,211 likes with 63.9k views (8.2% like rate) = extremely high engagement. The simplicity is the strategy.

**Tarun's use case:** any poem about love, grief, connection, or belonging. If the poem has a single perfect line — make that line the entire caption.

---

### FORMAT 4 — Text overlay on B-roll, no face
**Best example:** 35.5k "Maybe it's the feminine urge to romanticise everything"

**Visual:** Lush nature B-roll — flowers, greenery, natural light. The text is burned in as a centered overlay in a clean serif or display font.

**Audio:** Ambient/classical instrumental (Bryce Dessner "Opening" — or similar). NOT her speaking voice.

**Caption:** Full prose-poem (no stanza breaks, flowing lines). No hashtags.

**Why this format works:** fully face-free. It travels in pure visual feeds — people see the text before they see who made it. The aesthetic does the attracting; the poem does the retaining.

**Thumbnail text rule:** the first line of the poem IS the overlay text. It must work as a standalone phrase that stops scrolling:
- "Maybe it's the feminine urge to romanticise everything." ✅ — relatable, slightly self-aware, invites the viewer in
- "I've decided to believe in fairies again" ✅ — childlike and earnest, creates warmth before play is pressed

**Tarun's use case:** poems where the speaker doesn't need to be visible. Life/poetry pieces about nature, quiet emotions, internal states. No face needed = faster to produce.

---

### FORMAT 5 — Trending phrase subverted (Trojan horse)
**Best example:** 30.7k "Just Girly Things"

**Visual:** Lifestyle/aesthetic shot with a bold trending phrase burned in as overlay text (bubble-gum pink, playful font). Looks like viral meme content on first glance.

**Caption structure:**
```
[Narrative context — where the poem came from, 2–3 sentences]

[Invitation to participate — "try this prompt yourself, tag me"]

[Poem title]
[from the [event/context]]

[Full poem, stanzas separated by blank lines]
```

**The mechanics:** 
1. Use a trending meme phrase/aesthetic as the visual hook ("Just Girly Things," "That girl era," etc.)
2. The poem underneath SUBVERTS the surface meaning — the poem is weird, earthy, dark, specific
3. Caption invites participation: "take this prompt and write your own"
4. This format builds community — poets in her audience try it and tag her

**Why this works for growth:** the trending text attracts algorithm reach; the subversive poem converts the new viewer into a follower. You can only do this if the tension between surface and content is real — not manufactured.

## Structural elements of Christi's high-performing poems

### The "I would X but Y" structure (370k)
Three stanzas of impossible love gestures blocked by concrete reality, followed by a fourth stanza offering what IS possible. Creates:
- Escalating tenderness through repetition
- Surprise when the blocking reason is mundane or funny ("bird language is not on duolingo")
- Emotional payoff at the pivot: the limitation becomes the gift

### The rhetorical question close
"would the rivers whisper that I'm beautiful?" — not answered. The open question stays in the reader's head.

### Concrete nouns over abstract emotions
NEVER: "I feel so much love for you."  
ALWAYS: "I would plant flowers on your pillow / but that's not where flowers want to live."

The physical object grounds the emotion. Without the concrete noun, the poem is wallpaper.

### The playful line inside the tender poem
"bird language is not on duolingo" — humor embedded in a love poem. The tonal shift (earnest → playful → earnest again) creates the emotional texture that makes the poem feel alive, not greeting-card.

### The permission close
Poems that end in permission outperform those that end in sadness:
- "and you will never need a key" — you already have everything
- "just girly things." — this is normal, you're allowed to be this
- "and the air feels a little bit enchanted." — you're allowed to believe this

The close names the feeling and grants permission to have it. That is what gets screenshotted and sent.

## Hashtag strategy
**Default: no hashtags.** Her 370k, 63.9k, and 35.5k reels all have zero hashtags.

When used, hashtags serve a specific community:
- Spoken word / poetry community: `#poetry #spokenpoetry`
- Wedding/vows content: `#vows`
- Retreat/event content: `#poetryretreat #womensretreat #yogaretreat`

Never: `#love #instagood #viral #fyp` — these are not her audience's entry points.

---

## Audio strategy
- **Original audio (her voice):** Default for face-to-camera spoken word. The performance IS the content.
- **Ambient/classical instrumental:** For text-over-B-roll format. Bryce Dessner "Opening" or similar atmospheric pieces.
- **The audio for format 2 (hook line) must earn the view** — the performance has to justify "why couldn't I just read it in the caption?" It should feel different heard vs. read.

## What Tarun's Poetry content should steal

1. **Full poem in caption = the save trigger.** The caption is what people screenshot and send. If a poem is strong enough to stand alone — put it in the caption verbatim. The video is the delivery mechanism; the caption is what they keep.

2. **The concrete noun rule.** Tarun has a data scientist's precision — lean on it. "The probability of finding you in all possible futures is less than one." Physical, mathematical, specific. More memorable than "I would do anything for you."

3. **Humor inside tenderness.** The "bird language is not on duolingo" line is what gives "gifts" its texture. One unexpected mundane detail per poem prevents it from feeling constructed.

4. **Format 4 (text over B-roll) is low-effort, high-reach** — no face, no recording day needed. Write a poem, pick atmospheric footage, burn in the first line as text. Produces clean shareable content without a recording session.

5. **The permission close.** Tarun's poetry voice already tends toward resolution (10 years of data science = loves a conclusion). Lean into closing on permission: "you are allowed to be this." "and that is enough." "and the number doesn't lie."

6. **Single-line format for Love/Grief poems** — if the poem has one devastating line, make that line the entire caption over a beautiful visual. The simplicity is the point.

7. **Never explain the poem in the caption.** No "this poem is about X." No "I wrote this when Y happened." The poem explains itself, or it doesn't work. Context kills the projective magic.

## @joeykidney addendum — the forwarding mechanic and cinematic poetry

Joey Kidney operates poetry as a *hybrid* format: his poems are often indistinguishable from life content because they don't announce themselves as poems. No "poem by joeykidney." No title. The poem lands as a feeling, not a genre.

### The forwarding mechanic (209K likes — "I am proud you are my mother")

His single highest-engagement piece is 5 words: "I am proud you are my mother."

- Caption: "I am proud you are my mother" — nothing else
- Audio: Bon Iver "Wash." — emotionally saturated, recognizable
- Visual: bright daylight, close-up smile, white t-shirt
- Posted: 9 May = Mother's Day

The comments are people sending it: "My son just sent this to me," "@_nickcapone I am proud to call you MY son," "My baby boy is my whole world."

**The reel became a card.** Not content people engage with. Content people USE — as a gift to give their mother or child.

This is distinct from Christi's projective surface mechanism. Christi's poems make people feel seen. Joey's short-form statements make people want to *send it to someone*. Two different virality routes:

| Mechanism | Creator | Comment pattern | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Projective surface | Christi Steyn | "This is exactly my story…" | Share in their own feed |
| Forwarding card | Joey Kidney | "@mom" / "I sent this to him" | Tag or DM a specific person |

**The forwarding mechanic requires:**
1. A statement so simple it could have come from the viewer themselves
2. Emotional music that carries the weight the words don't spell out
3. A clean visual — bright, warm, no dark-cinematic aesthetic
4. Seasonal/relational context (Mother's Day, Valentine's, a friend anniversary)

### Short prose-poem in caption (the non-announcing poem)

"learning to unlove you / it took you seconds / people fall out of love in relationships but I don't think that is the saddest part / the saddest part is that they stay when all they want to do is leave"

This is a poem. He never calls it a poem. The caption is not labeled. It's formatted as prose paragraphs, not line breaks. The amber text overlay in the video is the visual poem ("to unlove you Learning took me years").

**The non-announcing poem format:**
- Write the poem as a prose argument
- Burn the core line as amber/gold text overlay on a cinematic close-up
- Caption = the poem without the title, stanza breaks, or "poem by" attribution
- Let the reader discover it's a poem through the rhythm and specificity of the language

This avoids the "poetry is a niche" algorithm penalty — the video looks like spoken life content and gets life-audience distribution, but reads as poetry to anyone paying attention.

### Cinematic aesthetic (dark, amber, intimate)

Joey's production quality is a constant comment topic: "Can I just say how incredible the videography and colour grading is on this video?!?!?"

**Signature elements:**
- Dark background with warm amber/red accent light hitting the face (film noir but intimate)
- Tight close-up — face fills 60–80% of frame
- Text overlay in amber or gold italic serif font
- Original audio (his voice, spoken conversationally — not performed/poetry-voice)
- Occasional prayer-hands or thoughtful pose as a visual anchor for heavy topics

This is not a podcast thumbnail. It's film-quality poetry delivery disguised as a talking-head.

### Seasonal timing as a virality multiplier

"I am proud you are my mother" on Mother's Day (9 May): 209K likes.
"I am proud to call you my mother" on 10 May (day after): 34.1K likes.

The same sentiment, run twice across the holiday window, captured both the holiday and the day-after audience. **The best forwarding-mechanic content aligns with relational calendar moments** — Mother's Day, Father's Day, Valentine's, friendship anniversaries, grief anniversaries.

**Tarun's use case:** write the poem year-round, but hold the forwarding-format pieces for the moment the audience is already in that emotional space.

## Comment sections as signal

The quality of comments tells you whether the formula landed:

| Comment type | What it means |
|---|---|
| "I accept 💚" (replying to a love poem offer) | Poem activated emotional identification — the reader became the speaker |
| "I met my partner 6 months ago and this is exactly…" | Full personal story projection — highest virality signal |
| "I teach English through your poetry" | Functional utility — poem is good enough to use |
| "You always touch my soul" | Aesthetic appreciation — good, but lower virality than personal story |
| "Just beautiful" | Lowest signal — common on mid-performers |

Aim for comment sections full of personal stories. That's the metric that means the poem reached through the screen.
