The quote 'dance like nobody's watching' has a dark sequel nobody tells you about:

Eventually, people watch.

And then the watching changes everything:

Last year I shot a 6-second video at 11pm. Alone in my apartment, doing something embarrassing enough that I almost deleted it before anyone saw.

I didn't delete it.

It became the most *me* thing I've ever put online.

Three years of making content taught me one pattern:

The pieces that land — the ones people reply to with 'this is exactly me' — were almost always the rough ones.

The ideas I shared mid-thought. The drafts I almost didn't post.

The word for this is unselfconsciousness.

Not a skill you build. A forgetting you practice.

Our brains are engineered to care. Dropping that — even briefly — might be the hardest creative act I know.

Here's the paradox:

The authentic act creates an audience.
The audience creates a mirror.
The mirror creates self-consciousness.

Suddenly the dance is choreographed.

It wasn't supposed to be.

What I've found that helps: one thing per week with no audience at all.

Not for a post. Not as a draft. Not to revisit.

Write a poem nobody sees. Build something to see if you can. Move before you know where you're going.

The unobserved moments keep your center of gravity low.

They remind you what it feels like to begin without permission.

When the observed moments come — the posts, the reels — they carry residue of that. You can feel the difference.

There's a draft sitting in your Notes right now.

The room won't clear. The applause won't arrive in advance.

Self-consciousness only disappears inside the doing.

Dance anyway.
