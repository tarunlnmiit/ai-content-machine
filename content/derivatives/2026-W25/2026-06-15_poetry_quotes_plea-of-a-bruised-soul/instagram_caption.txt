The wrong kind of comfort doesn't look like cruelty. It looks like care.

It rushes in. It reframes. It offers silver linings before the wound has finished bleeding.

And the person on the receiving end learns to stop saying how they really feel — because honesty, when it's met with solutions, becomes a burden.

This poem is for anyone who has ever needed to be witnessed. Not fixed. Not framed. Not quietly told that their grief has become inconvenient.

Just seen. Exactly as they are.

If it finds you in a heavy week — keep it close. Or send it to someone who needs to feel less alone in their fracture.
