The hardest thing you can offer someone in pain isn't advice — it's your full, undivided presence in a room that doesn't feel good to sit in.

Most of us were never taught to be with pain. We were taught to solve it.

So when someone shows us their fracture, our instinct is to hand them cement. Reframe it. Name the lesson. Point toward the exit. It comes from care — but it can leave the other person feeling more alone than before the conversation started.

I've been on both sides of this. I've stayed too long in solution mode when someone only needed quiet. And I've received wisdom that felt like dismissal dressed in kindness.

The distinction that changed how I show up for people:

Witness says — I see that this is real, and I am not leaving because of it.

Rescue says — let me make this something other than what it is, so we can both feel better sooner.

One stays in the room. The other quietly implies you should be somewhere else by now.
