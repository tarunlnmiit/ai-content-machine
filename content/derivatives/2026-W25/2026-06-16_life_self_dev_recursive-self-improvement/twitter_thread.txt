Most self-improvement is a treadmill.

Read the book. Start the habit. Fall off. Repeat.

What nobody tells you: there's a second-order game underneath — where getting better at *getting better* is the real unlock.

Here are 5 loops that make improvement self-sustaining:

Loop 1: Fix your information diet before fixing your habits.

Most productivity content gives you motivation, not models.

Motivation fades after a weekend. A better mental model changes every decision afterward.

Stop consuming updates. Start consuming frameworks.

Loop 2: Build habit stacks, not habits.

A single habit maintains momentum — it doesn't generate it.

When one behavior cues another, each link makes the next one cheaper to install.

For most people the anchor is physical. Movement upstream makes every cognitive habit downstream more likely to hold.

Loop 3: Build a review system that reviews itself.

Most people journal the same way for years. Same prompts. Same blind spots.

Once a quarter: review your reviews. Where were you consistently wrong about yourself?

This teaches you how you think — not just what you think.

Loop 4: Find the skills that unlock other skills.

Skills aren't flat. They exist in a network. Some nodes have far more edges than others.

Writing clearly → thinking clearly → learning any domain faster.

High-leverage skills look soft. That's exactly why most people deprioritize them.

Loop 5: Rewrite the identity narrative before the behavior.

Behavior follows identity.

If your self-concept doesn't update as you improve, you'll unconsciously self-sabotage back to whoever you used to be.

The recursive question: who are you becoming — and what one behavior proves it today?

All 5 loops share one trait: they don't make you better in isolation — they make the *process of getting better* more accurate and self-sustaining.

The question worth sitting with: at what level are you currently optimizing?
