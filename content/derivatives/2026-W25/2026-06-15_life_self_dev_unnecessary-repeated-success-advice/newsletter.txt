Subject: The advice is right. The timing is wrong.
Preview: Why you keep hearing things you already know — and what that actually means.

There's a pattern I've noticed in myself that I suspect you've lived too.

You hear a piece of advice. You understand it. You file it away. Two years later, in a completely different context, someone says the exact same thing — and it cracks something open.

Same advice. Different you.

What I've come to believe: advice doesn't stick because of willpower or finding the right framework. It sticks when it arrives close to real lived experience. When the soil is ready.

And the uncomfortable part: you can't rush that. You can't read your way into readiness. Some things are waiting for you on the other side of an experience you haven't had yet.

This week's piece is about the $13B self-improvement loop, why consumption becomes a substitute for discomfort, and what actually makes advice land — including specific moments from my own life where timing made all the difference.
